# Bahi: product case

For Ramesh, 44, in `persona.md`.

## 1. The problem
"Sharma ji owes four thousand since Diwali. If I ask, he goes to the app. If I do not ask, my money is stuck and
the distributor is at my door."

## 2. Jobs to be done
- Let me say "Sharma ji, 340" and have it written, with no typing.
- Ask for my money on the right day, politely, so I do not have to.
- Tell me each morning who owes the most for the longest, and whom to stop.

## 3. What it is not for
- Not billing, stock or GST.
- Not lending. It does not give Ramesh or his customers a loan.
- Not a collection agent. One reminder on the due day and one a week later. No more.

## 4. The number
**Days from giving credit to being paid.** Target: down by a third in 60 days.

Guardrails that must not get worse:
- Credit customers lost after a reminder stay under 1 in 50.
- An entry takes under 5 seconds, or he goes back to the notebook.
- A customer hears only his own dues, never anyone else's.

## 5. The riskiest assumption
A reminder from an app, in the shopkeeper's voice, makes customers pay sooner and does not make them feel insulted.
Second risk: Khatabook and OkCredit already do typed ledgers with reminders. Voice entry and a spoken reminder may
not be enough reason to switch.

## 6. Test with 5 real shopkeepers
- Who: 5 small shopkeepers who give credit, of whom at least 2 have tried a ledger app and left it. sumeet recruits
  them.
- How: clickable screens for entry. For reminders, each shopkeeper records one voice note and sends it himself on
  WhatsApp to 5 customers who owe him.
- Pass: 4 of 5 make ten entries by voice without help, and at least 10 of the 25 customers pay within a week with
  no complaint.
- If it fails: if entry works and reminders upset people, keep the ledger and drop the reminders. If the two who
  left other apps see nothing new, stop.
