# Ramesh Gupta, 44 (Business owners and shopkeepers)

Fictional. The numbers are real and listed in `../sources.md`.

## Who he is
- Runs a kirana shop in a housing colony in Indore, Madhya Pradesh. His father opened it in 1989.
- His wife sits at the counter in the afternoons. A son is in Class 12 and a daughter in college.
- Sales are about Rs 9,000 a day. He keeps about Rs 30,000 a month after rent and stock.
- He takes UPI and has two apps from distributors. His credit book is a paper notebook.

## His day
Open at 7, shut at 10. The distributor's man wants payment on delivery or within a week. About 60 families buy on
credit and pay "after salary". Some pay on the 5th, some after three reminders, and four have moved away owing him.
He does not like to ask. Asking loses the customer to the quick delivery apps.

## What is hard
- His money sits in other people's homes. Across India an estimated Rs 8.1 lakh crore is stuck in delayed payments
  to small businesses (white paper by the Women's Collective Forum with National Law University, February 2026).
  That figure is for all 7.16 crore registered MSMEs, mostly business to business. We found no checked figure for
  kirana credit alone, so the size of his problem is from the persona, not from data.
- He cannot say at any moment how much he is owed in total, or by whom for how long.
- Quick delivery apps have taken his best customers. Credit is the one thing he gives that they do not.
- He has no time to learn a new app, and no wish to type.

## His biggest problem
He gives credit to keep customers and then cannot collect it without hurting the same relationship.

## The app
**Bahi.** He says the entry aloud. On the due day the customer gets a polite reminder in Ramesh's own voice, so he
does not have to ask. See `case.md`.
