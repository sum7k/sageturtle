# Bhav: product case

For Balwant, 47, in `persona.md`.

## 1. The problem
"The trader says forty-two hundred. Somebody says Vidisha mandi is giving forty-five. By the time I find out, I
have already sold."

## 2. Jobs to be done
- Tell me today's price for my crop at the mandis I can reach.
- Tell me what I would keep at each one after the trolley and the day lost.
- Tell me if the price has been rising or falling this week, so I can decide to wait.

## 3. What it is not for
- Not a place to sell. It does not buy, broker or find buyers.
- Not crop advice, weather or loans.
- It does not promise tomorrow's price. It says what happened, not what will.

## 4. The number
**Farmers who sold above the first price they were offered.** Asked by voice after each sale. Target: half of those
who checked before selling.

Guardrails that must not get worse:
- A spoken price is never more than one day old. If it is, the app says so.
- Nobody is sent to a mandi where the net after transport is lower than the village offer.
- Works on a weak signal and a cheap phone. An answer in under 10 seconds.

## 5. The riskiest assumption
Knowing the price changes where or when he sells. He owes the trader money, and that may bind him whatever the
price. Second risk: the government's daily mandi prices (Agmarknet, eNAM) are right and fresh enough for his crop
and his mandis.

## 6. Test with 5 real farmers
- Who: 5 farmers with under 5 acres who have a crop to sell in the next month. sumeet recruits them.
- How: no app needed. Each morning for 10 days, sumeet sends each farmer a 20 second voice note with the price at
  3 mandis and the net after transport, taken from Agmarknet.
- Pass: 4 of 5 listen on most days, and 2 of 5 use the figure to sell elsewhere, wait, or get a better price from
  the trader.
- If it fails: if they listened and could not act, the block is the debt to the trader, and price alone is not the
  product. Stop and look at that instead.
