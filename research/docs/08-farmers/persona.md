# Balwant Singh Lodhi, 47 (Farmers and rural)

Fictional. The numbers are real and listed in `../sources.md`.

## Who he is
- Farms 1.8 acres in a village in Vidisha district, Madhya Pradesh. Soybean in kharif, wheat in rabi.
- His wife works the farm with him. One son works at a factory in Mandideep and sends money. A daughter is in Class
  9.
- Studied to Class 7. Has a basic smartphone, used for calls, WhatsApp voice notes and videos.
- Owes Rs 60,000 on a Kisan Credit Card and Rs 35,000 to the village trader, who also sells him seed and fertiliser.

## His day
In the field from 6. At harvest the trader's tractor comes to the village and offers a price. The mandi is 28 km
away and costs a day and Rs 1,200 for a trolley. He hears prices from neighbours a day late. He sells to the trader
within a week because the loan is due.

## What is hard
- His land is the national average and shrinking: 0.74 hectares, down from 1.08 in five years (NABARD NAFIS
  2021-22).
- A farm household earns Rs 13,661 a month and spends Rs 11,710, leaving Rs 1,951. Only about a third of the income
  comes from farming itself (NAFIS 2021-22).
- Average debt of a farm household is Rs 91,231. About a quarter of rural loans still come from outside banks
  (NAFIS 2021-22).
- He does not know today's price at the three mandis in reach, or what he would keep after transport.
- The trader is also his lender, so the price he is offered is not a free one.

## His biggest problem
He sells without knowing the price. A difference of Rs 200 a quintal on 15 quintals is more than his whole monthly
surplus.

## The app
**Bhav.** He asks aloud, and it tells him today's price for his crop at the nearest mandis and what he would keep
after transport. See `case.md`.
