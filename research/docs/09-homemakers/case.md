# Haath: product case

For Kavita, 36, in `persona.md`.

## 1. The problem
"Everyone says my achar should be sold. To whom? I cannot stand in a market. I have two hours in the day and I want
something that is mine."

## 2. Jobs to be done
- Let the homes near me know what I make and when I can make it.
- Take orders only in the hours I set, and only as many as I can do.
- Let the money come to me, in my name.

## 3. What it is not for
- Not delivery. Buyers walk over and collect.
- Not a marketplace for the whole city. It reaches a few hundred metres.
- Not a job. Nobody sets her targets, ratings or hours.

## 4. The number
**Homemakers who earn their first Rs 1,000 within 30 days.** Target: 1 in 4.

Guardrails that must not get worse:
- Her flat number is shown only to a buyer whose order she has accepted.
- Orders never arrive outside the hours she set.
- Payment goes to an account or UPI in her own name.

## 5. The riskiest assumption
Neighbours will order from a home they do not already know, through an app, and come to collect. Second risk: her
family is at ease with strangers at the door and with her earning.

## 6. Test with 5 real homemakers
- Who: 5 homemakers who already make or do something others praise. sumeet recruits them.
- How: no app needed. For each, make one picture card (what, price, hours) and post it in her own building or lane
  WhatsApp group. Run for 2 weeks.
- Pass: 3 of 5 get at least 3 paid orders from people outside family and close friends, and none wants to stop for
  reasons of safety or family.
- If it fails: if orders came only from friends, the reach is the problem, so test a wider radius. If families
  objected, the block is at home and no app fixes it.
