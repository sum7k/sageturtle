# Imran Ansari, 24 (Job seekers)

Fictional. The numbers are real and listed in `../sources.md`.

## Who he is
- BCom from a college in Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh, two years ago. Lives with his parents and two sisters.
- Father is a tailor with a small shop. Family income is about Rs 18,000 a month.
- He prepares for SSC and bank exams in a library that charges Rs 500 a month, and applies to private jobs on the
  side.
- He is on four job apps and a dozen Telegram and WhatsApp job groups.

## His day
Library from 9 to 5. In between, his phone brings 10 to 15 job messages a day: "data entry, work from home,
Rs 25,000", "HR calling, interview tomorrow", "registration fee Rs 999". He cannot tell which are real. Last year he
paid Rs 2,500 as a "security deposit" for a job that did not exist. He has told nobody at home.

## What is hard
- Being educated has not helped. Graduate unemployment was 29.1% against 3.4% for people who cannot read (ILO India
  Employment Report 2024, data for 2022).
- 93% of Indian job seekers have met a suspicious or fake job offer, and 51% say they cannot tell a real recruiter
  from a fraud (Indeed, 2026).
- 75% now ignore offers out of fear, and 14% know they have missed a real one that way (Indeed, 2026). He does the
  same.
- Among Gen Z seekers in that survey, 46% reported losing money.
- Each failed exam and each fake offer makes it harder to face his father.

## His biggest problem
He cannot tell a real job offer from a trap. So he either loses money or throws away real chances.

## The app
**Parakh.** He forwards any job message, call or offer letter and hears in a minute whether it is real, and why.
See `case.md`.
