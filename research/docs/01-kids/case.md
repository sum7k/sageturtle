# Kahani: product case

For Pihu, 8, in `persona.md`.

## 1. The problem
"I can say the letters, but the story does not come. Mummy is busy. Bhaiya laughs when I get stuck."

## 2. Jobs to be done
- Let me read a story aloud to someone who waits for me and does not laugh.
- When I am stuck on a word, say it for me and let me go on.
- Give me a story that is just a little harder than yesterday's.

## 3. What it is not for
- Not a school, not homework, not maths.
- No videos, no games, no scores, no ranks, no rewards to collect.
- Not for the parent to watch the child. The parent only hears one line a week: "she read 6 stories".

## 4. The number
**Stories read aloud to the end, per child per week.** Target: 5.

Guardrails that must not get worse:
- Time on the phone stays under 15 minutes a day. The app closes itself.
- Stories given up halfway stay under 1 in 4. More than that means the level is wrong.
- Nothing is recorded or kept beyond the phone.

## 5. The riskiest assumption
A child will read aloud to a phone, alone, day after day, with no adult pushing and no reward. If this is false,
nothing else matters. Second risk: the phone can hear a child's Hindi well enough to know which word she is stuck
on.

## 6. Test with 5 real children
- Who: 5 children in Classes 2 to 4 who cannot yet read a Class 2 text. sumeet recruits them.
- How: the clickable screens on one phone. sumeet sits beside the child and plays the listener himself by tapping
  the stuck word. No speech software is needed for the test.
- Do: leave the phone with the family for 5 evenings. Do not remind them.
- Pass: 3 of 5 children read at least 3 stories to the end on their own, and at least 3 ask for it again.
- If it fails: try the same stories with an elder sibling as the listener and the phone only showing the text. If
  that works, the product is for the sibling, not the child.
