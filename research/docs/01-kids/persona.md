# Pihu Yadav, 8 (Kids 6 to 12)

Fictional. The numbers are real and listed in `../sources.md`.

## Who she is
- Class 3 in the government primary school of a village in Sitapur district, Uttar Pradesh.
- Father is a mason who works in Lucknow through the week. Mother is an anganwadi helper. One elder brother, 11.
- Speaks Awadhi at home. School is in Hindi.
- The house has one smartphone, her mother's. Pihu gets it for about an hour after dinner.

## Her day
School from 8 to 2 with 40 children in one room. She copies from the board neatly but cannot read most of it back.
After school she fetches water, plays, and watches cartoons and songs on the phone until it is taken away.

## What is hard
- She knows her letters but cannot read a simple story. In rural government schools only 23.4% of Class 3 children
  can read a Class 2 text (ASER 2024).
- She cannot do subtraction with borrowing. Only 27.6% of Class 3 children in government schools can (ASER 2024).
- Nobody at home has time to sit and listen to her read. Her mother studied to Class 8 and is busy until night.
- The phone hour goes to videos. 66% of urban parents of 9 to 17 year olds say their child is hooked on social
  media, OTT or games (LocalCircles 2024). The pull in the village is the same.

## Her biggest problem
Nobody listens to her read. Reading grows only by reading aloud to someone patient, every day, and she has no such
person.

## The app
**Kahani.** A phone that listens while she reads a short story aloud, and helps on the word where she gets stuck.
See `case.md`.
