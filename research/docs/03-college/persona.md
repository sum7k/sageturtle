# Sneha Patil, 20 (College students)

Fictional. The numbers are real and listed in `../sources.md`.

## Who she is
- Second year BA (Economics) at a government-aided college in a taluka town of Kolhapur district, Maharashtra.
- Father runs a small sugarcane farm. Mother is in a self-help group. A younger brother is in Class 10.
- First woman in her family to reach college. She travels 22 km by state bus each way.
- Speaks Marathi. Reads English slowly and is shy to speak it.

## Her day
Three lectures, of which one usually does not happen. Notes are photocopies from seniors. Afternoons she teaches
four neighbourhood children for Rs 1,500 a month. Evenings she watches "how to get a job" videos and feels behind.

## What is hard
- Her degree does not show what she can do. Of India's 3.4 crore undergraduates, 1.13 crore are in BA, and only
  8.25% of graduates work in a job that matches their degree (NITI Aayog, reported August 2026).
- Only 54.81% of graduates test as employable (India Skills Report 2025).
- She has never worked for an employer. Her college has no placement cell and no internships.
- A plain degree starts at about Rs 22,000 a month. With some proven skill it is about Rs 29,000 (BCG survey cited
  by NITI Aayog).
- Courses online give certificates, but no employer near her has asked for one.

## Her biggest problem
She will graduate with nothing to show an employer except marks. She has never done one piece of real work that
someone outside college has checked.

## The app
**Namuna.** Small real tasks from nearby employers. She does one, the employer says what was good and what was not,
and it becomes proof she can show. See `case.md`.
