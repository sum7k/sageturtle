# Raah: product case

For Arjun, 16, in `persona.md`.

## 1. The problem
"Everyone asks what I will become. I say government job. I do not know what else there is, and there is nobody I
can ask."

## 2. Jobs to be done
- Show me people like me, from places like mine, and what they do for a living now.
- Tell me what it pays, what it needed, and the first step they took at my age.
- Let me keep the two or three paths I like, and tell me the next step for each.

## 3. What it is not for
- Not a job board and not exam coaching.
- Not a chat with strangers. A teen can send a question, and the answer comes back as a story for everyone.
- Not a personality test that tells him what he "is".

## 4. The number
**Teens who, after two weeks, can name one job and the next step toward it.** Asked by voice in the app. Target:
half of those who listened to five stories.

Guardrails that must not get worse:
- No direct contact between a teen and an adult storyteller.
- Time in the app stays under 20 minutes a day.
- Every pay figure in a story is checked. No story sells a course.

## 5. The riskiest assumption
A 16 year old will choose to listen to a real person's work story when reels are one tap away. Second risk: hearing
a path changes what he does next, and is not just interesting.

## 6. Test with 5 real teens
- Who: 5 students in Classes 9 to 12 who cannot name a job they want. sumeet recruits them.
- How: clickable screens with 8 real stories recorded on a phone, 90 seconds each.
- Do: ask "what will you do after school?" before. Hand over the phone for 15 minutes. Ask again a week later.
- Pass: 4 of 5 listen to at least 3 stories without being told to, and 3 of 5 name a job and a first step a week
  later.
- If it fails: test the same stories played by a teacher to a whole class. If that works, it belongs with teachers.
