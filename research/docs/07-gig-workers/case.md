# Hisaab: product case

For Suresh, 29, in `persona.md`.

## 1. The problem
"The app says eleven hundred. My pocket says something else. At the end of the month I do not know where it went or
which days were worth it."

## 2. Jobs to be done
- Tell me each night what I really kept today, after petrol, cuts and repairs.
- Tell me each week which hours and which app paid best per hour.
- Show me every cut, so I can ask about the ones I do not understand.

## 3. What it is not for
- Not a place to find work.
- Not a loan, an advance or insurance.
- Not a union or a complaints channel to the platforms. It gives him the facts to complain with.

## 4. The number
**Workers who close 5 or more days a week.** Closing a day means the night figure was made and heard. Target: half
of those who start.

Guardrails that must not get worse:
- Closing a day takes under 30 seconds: a photo of the payout screen and one spoken line for petrol.
- His earnings are never shared with a platform, lender or anyone else.
- It never tells him to ride longer.

## 5. The riskiest assumption
A man who has ridden 12 hours will give 30 seconds every night, for weeks, to learn a number that may be bad news.
Second risk: a photo of the payout screen can be read correctly across apps.

## 6. Test with 5 real workers
- Who: 5 delivery or cab workers, or daily-wage workers with more than one source of work. sumeet recruits them.
- How: no app needed. For 7 nights each worker sends sumeet a photo of the payout screen and a voice note on petrol
  and costs. sumeet replies with the one-sentence figure, and on day 7 with the best hours.
- Pass: 4 of 5 send on at least 5 of 7 nights, and 3 of 5 say they will change an hour or an app because of it.
- If it fails: try once a week instead of every night. If that fails too, the figure is not wanted enough.
