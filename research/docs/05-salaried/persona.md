# Priya Nair, 31 (Salaried professionals)

Fictional. The numbers are real and listed in `../sources.md`.

## Who she is
- Operations executive at a logistics company in Pune. From Thrissur, Kerala. Married, with a 3 year old daughter.
- Take-home pay is Rs 48,000 a month. Her husband, a sales executive, brings home Rs 40,000.
- They rent a one-bedroom flat for Rs 16,000. She sends Rs 8,000 a month to her parents.
- EMIs: a scooter and a personal loan taken for her father's bypass surgery, Rs 11,500 together.

## Her day
Out at 8, back at 8. Daycare pickup is shared. Salary comes on the 1st and is spoken for by the 5th. On the 25th
she pays the last bills by credit card. Her company laid off 40 people in March.

## What is hard
- One bad month would sink them. 55% of salaried employees are not prepared to meet expenses if they lose the job
  (Finsafe, FY2025-26), and nearly 7 in 10 could last six months or less (Tata AIG, 2026).
- Only 31% have both an emergency fund and enough insurance (Finsafe).
- Her parents are not on her company's health cover. 45% of employees rely only on the employer's cover, and about
  75% say it would not be enough in an emergency (Tata AIG, 2026).
- Up to half of income goes to rent or EMIs and household costs (Grant Thornton, 2026). For her it is more.
- Advice is everywhere, and all of it is selling her something.

## Her biggest problem
She does not know how long her family would be safe if her salary stopped or a parent fell ill, and nobody she
trusts will tell her the one thing to do first.

## The app
**Kavach.** One picture: how many months the family is safe. And one step this month to make it longer. It sells
nothing. See `case.md`.
