# Shell: tradeoffs

Choices made without asking. Only the current choice is kept.

Format: `- [where] Choice. Why. Cost / what we gave up.`

- [order] The first run goes language, lock, PIN code, what you do, alerts, Home. Why: language comes first so every later screen can speak it, and the lock comes second so the account exists before the person gives any detail. Cost: the fingerprint is asked before the person has seen anything useful.
- [account] The passkey is the whole account. No phone number, no OTP, no password. Why: you said the first open sets biometrics, nothing has to be typed, and there is no SMS cost. Cost: a person who loses the phone gets the account back only if the phone backs up its passkeys (Google or iCloud does this by default). People find each other by username or QR code, not by phone number. You confirmed this on 2026-11-26: no phone number.
- [lock] The lock is asked at set-up, on a profile or variant change, and before a payment. It is not asked on every open. Why: the phone's own lock already guards the app, and a daily prompt tires people. Cost: anyone holding the unlocked phone can open the app.
- [profiles] Entering or leaving a child's profile asks for the owner's fingerprint. Cost: a phone cannot tell whose finger it is. If the child's finger is also saved on that phone, the child passes. The owner must be told this when adding a child.
- [select] A tap on a block speaks and selects it. The gold tick goes on. Why: a person who cannot read must hear a choice before making it. Cost: one more tap on each screen.
- [pincode] The PIN code can be tapped, spoken, or found by location, and the place name is shown and spoken to check it. Cost: Find me opens the phone's location prompt.
- [alerts] Notifications are asked on their own screen, with a "Not now". Why: Khabri is pushed, and iPhone allows the question only in an installed app after a tap. Cost: one more screen in the first run.
- [home] Home is a Khabri card, then the app tiles three in a row, with a gold speak button as the bottom centre cell. No bottom bar. The round picture at the top right opens Me, so Me has no tile. Why: Khabri binds the variants, and Khabri's frame is bare. Cost: eleven tiles and the speak button fill a small phone, so nothing else fits on Home.
- [home] Human: the default tile order follows Cheiro's numbers on a phone keypad (2026-11-27): 1 Earnings (Sun), 2 Wisdom (Moon, paired with Neptune's 7), 3 Meetings (Jupiter), 4 Discussions (Uranus), 5 Learning (Mercury, the centre), 6 Hobbies (Venus), 7 Messages (Neptune), 8 Reminders (Saturn), 9 Events (Mars); bottom row Directory, the mic, Adverts. Why: sumeet wanted Learning at the centre, Directory and Adverts at the bottom, and numbers that suit each app; the centre cell is also the one looked at longest, and the bottom centre is easiest for the thumb. Cost: Cheiro's numbers describe people and dates, not screen places; Meetings at 3 is a weak match; Messages loses Mercury's 5 to Learning. Picture: `apps/shell/work/home-grid-order.png`.
- [home] No ads on Home. Why: the first screen stays calm, and ads sit inside the apps. Cost: less ad space. You confirmed this on 2026-11-26: no ads on Home.
- [voice] One "Press and speak" button on Home opens any app or asks a question. Every screen has a Listen pill. Cost: this needs speech in and speech out in 12 languages, and I have not priced it.
- [text] Your rule is no written text. I read it as: no words at all before the language is chosen, and after that no written instructions, headings or button words. Language names in their own script, numbers, names of people, places and apps, and content such as a khabar stay as text. Why: a name under a picture helps a person who can read and harms nobody. Cost: the screens are not fully free of text. You confirmed on 2026-11-26 that headings and button words stay off the inner screens.
- [landing] The landing page has no words and no name, only the turtle on the hill, 12 small app pictures and the install cue. Why: the language is not known yet. Cost: a first-time visitor is not told in writing what the app is. The speaker button and the pictures carry it.
- [landing] The page shows one of three install pictures by browser. Android and Chrome get a gold install button. iPhone Safari gets an arrow to Safari's three-dot button and a strip of the taps. Another app's browser gets an arrow to its menu and "open in browser". Why: Safari has no install box, and in-app browsers cannot install. Cost: the page must detect the browser and its version. Apple moved the share button into the three-dot menu in iOS 26, so the picture can go out of date when a browser changes its bar.
- [landing] The speaker line plays in the phone's language if it is one of the 12, else in Hindi and then English. Cost: a person whose phone is set to another language hears two languages.
- [language] Each language block shows only the name in its own script. The phone's language is selected at the start. Cost: a helper who reads only English cannot find a language by name.
- [variant] The nine blocks carry a picture and a plain name ("Farming", "Daily work", "Home"), not the variant names. Why: people know what they do, not our labels. Cost: "Home" for homemakers and "Daily work" for gig workers may need better words in each language.
- [cue] A drawn hand points at the main button on the landing and lock screens. Why: with no words, a first-time user needs to see where to tap. Cost: it must be animated in the real app to read as "tap here".
- [drawing] The screens after the language step are drawn in English, for the Farmers variant, with simple line icons. Why: fastest way to judge the flow. Cost: the real pictures in Khabri's drawn style are still to be made.
- [child] Adding a child takes a picture, a spoken name and one of two blocks, 6-12 or 13-17. The gold tick asks for the owner's fingerprint. Why: three things are enough to start a profile. Cost: no birth date, so a child does not move from Kids to Teens by itself.
- [listening] "Press and speak" opens a dark full screen with moving bars, the words as they are heard, and the gold mic to finish. Why: the person must see that the phone is listening and what it heard. Cost: showing the words needs live speech-to-text.
- [offline] With no network, Home shows a no-network picture with a try-again button, the last saved khabar, and dims the tiles that need the network. Why: the person sees what still works. Cost: each app must say what it can do offline. In the drawing I guessed three (Farm School, Crop Calendar, Voice Messages).
- [night] Night colours are drawn for Home only: navy ground, lighter navy cards, gold unchanged, dark versions of the four tints, and a moon in place of the sun. Why: one screen is enough to judge the palette. Cost: the other screens are not drawn at night yet.
- [khabri] Khabri is drawn on the shell canvas for now (four screens, row 3). Why: it shares the frame and colours, and one canvas is easier to review. Cost: it moves to its own app folder when Khabri is built.
- [khabri] Inside the shell, Khabri loses its gear and its You page. The reader's facts live in Me, and the day items (panchang, rashi, number, seasonal note) come as one card in the deck. Why: one place for facts about the person. Cost: the day items are no longer one tap away.
- [khabri] A swipe shows a gold heart for "I like this"; "not for me" shows nothing. Why: calm, no negative mark. Cost: the right swipe has no visible confirmation.
- [khabri] Human: no levels to choose (2026-11-26). Each khabar is one piece that starts with the easiest part and adds detail as the reader goes on. Why: nothing to pick, the reader just reads. Cost: an expert scrolls past the easy start; the per-topic level memory is dropped.
- [me] The round picture on Home and in Khabri opens Me, not Profiles. Profiles is now the Family tile inside Me. Why: Me is the one place for everything about the person. Cost: switching to a child's profile takes one more tap.
- [me] Me is eight picture tiles, each showing its present value. Five open the same screen used at set-up (language, what I do, place, alerts, lock). Why: nothing new to learn. Cost: no room for more settings without a second row of screens.
- [me] Child control has four things: time each day, sleep time, guardians, and which apps are on. Why: the smallest set a parent needs. Cost: no per-app time limits or activity report yet.
- [me] Remove ads shows only the price, 365 days and a UPI code; the fingerprint is asked before paying. Why: matches the 1,200 rupees a year plan through Dodo. Cost: no monthly option.
- [me] Birthday is kept in Me (it was in Khabri's You page) for the rashi and day card. Why: one place for facts. Cost: tradition and calendar choice are not drawn yet.
- [networks] Human: a person keeps separate lists of people (2026-11-26). Agreed set: family, friends, work, trade, neighbours and class. Why: each has its own trust and its own things to share. Cost: more to manage than one contact list.
- [networks] One person can be in more than one list, and a variant shows only the lists that fit it (a farmer has no work list, a kid sees only guardians). Why: real life overlaps, and unused lists are noise. Cost: the rule for which variant gets which list is still to be written in the map.
- [networks] People join a list only by scanning each other's code. Why: no search by name or number, so strangers cannot find anyone. Cost: people must meet, or send the code picture another way.
- [class] Human agreed 2026-11-26: a class is one shared group seen through each role. The teacher makes it and people join by the class code. Teacher: students, speak to all, homework, call, office hours. Parent: the class sits under the child, a fingerprint lets a teacher in, other parents are visible. Kid: manages nothing, the teacher is a guardian. Teen: classmates and a class chat the teacher can see. Why: one object, no duplicate groups. Cost: a parent with children in many classes has many class pages.
- [class] For kids, a teacher's message to the class goes to the parents. Why: kids talk only to guardians and many cannot read. Cost: the parent must pass it on.
- [handoff] Human: one-to-one calls and messages are handed off by link to the phone's dialer, SMS, WhatsApp or Telegram (2026-11-26). No APIs and no fees. Why: these already work for 100 crore people, and we build nothing that cannot scale. Cost: the talk happens outside BodhiLabs, so there is no history, no report button and no office hours there, and the other person sees the caller's number.
- [handoff] Human: each person may fill Reach me in Me: one mobile number and one app for messages (WhatsApp, Telegram or SMS). It is optional and never checked by OTP (sumeet chose this on 2026-11-28, over the phone's own contacts and over no hand-off). Why: a link needs a number, it works on every phone and it costs nothing. The number is a contact detail, not the account, so the no-phone-number rule for the account still holds. Cost: BodhiLabs now stores a mobile number, an unchecked number can be wrong, and a person who leaves it empty cannot be reached from a row.
- [handoff] A row has two buttons: a handset for the dialer and the chosen app's mark for a message. Why: WhatsApp has no public link that starts a call, only one that opens the chat. Cost: no one-tap WhatsApp call; the person taps the call button inside WhatsApp.
- [handoff] Telegram opens by number (`t.me/+<number>`), not by username. Why: nothing more to type. Cost: it fails for a person who hid their number in Telegram's privacy settings; a username field can be added then.
- [handoff] WhatsApp and Telegram are shown by their own marks, green and blue, off our palette. Why: a person who cannot read knows these marks. Cost: two foreign colours on the screen, and the final marks must follow each brand's rules.
- [handoff] Kids have no Reach me and are never handed off. Why: WhatsApp needs age 13, and a kid may talk only to guardians, which no outside app can enforce. Cost: in-app calls and messages must be built for Kids (voice later on Cloudflare Realtime).
- [handoff] A teen's Reach me is filled by the guardian in child control, with phone, SMS and WhatsApp only. Why: WhatsApp allows 13; I could not confirm Telegram's age rule for India. Cost: a teen cannot be reached on Telegram from here.
- [handoff] Groups stay inside the app: speak to the class, the class chat a teacher can see, and the class call. Why: a link opens one person's chat, and the class rules (teacher sees, office hours, kids' messages go to parents) cannot hold in an outside group. Cost: group messages and calls are still to be built.
