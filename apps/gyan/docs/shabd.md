# शब्द (Word): from sounds to literature

Written 2026-11-28. A design document; nothing here is built. It answers sumeet's question: will a child learn to
read from "see a picture, pick the written word"? Short answer: no, not by that alone. Research says what must come
before and after, and it fits the ring he described.

## 1. What research says

**Reading is two things multiplied.** Reading with understanding = turning print into sound (decoding) × understanding
the spoken language. A child weak in either cannot read with meaning (the "simple view of reading", Gough and Tunmer
1986). So शब्द must build both, all the way up.

**The order that works** (US National Reading Panel 2000, and later reviews):

1. Spoken language: many words, sentences, stories heard.
2. Hearing the sounds inside words (syllables, first sound, rhyme), with no print.
3. Letters and their sounds, taught one by one in a planned order, and joined into words at once. This beats learning
   whole words by their shape.
4. Fluency: reading aloud, again and again, until it is quick and correct.
5. Vocabulary and understanding: longer texts, questions, talk about what was read.
6. Writing runs beside reading from step 3. Writing a letter helps a child remember it.

**Indian scripts are different from English.** The unit is the akshara (a syllable such as का), not a single letter.

- There are few sounds to learn but many shapes: a child meets over 400 common consonant and vowel-sign combinations,
  and children are still learning the harder ones in class 3 or 4 (Nag 2007, Kannada; read through a summary).
- Children hear syllables first and single sounds much later. So sound games should work with syllables.
- Easy to hard: plain aksharas (क, म), then vowel signs (का, कि), then joined letters (क्य, स्त).
- Sound and script should be taught together, not as two tracks.

**India's numbers** (ASER 2024, rural):

- Only 23.4% of class 3 children can read a class 2 story (20.9% in 2018, 16.3% in 2022).
- In government schools, 44.8% in class 5 and 67.5% in class 8 can read it.
- ASER's ladder is: cannot read letters, letters, words, a class 1 paragraph, a class 2 story.

**NIPUN Bharat** (launched 5 July 2021, target 2026-27) sets the national goal: pre-school children know letters and
their sounds and read 2 to 3 letter words; class 1 reads short sentences; class 2 reads 45 to 60 words a minute with
meaning; class 3 reads at least 60.

**Teaching at the Right Level** (Pratham): children are tested with the ASER ladder, grouped by what they can do and
not by their class, and taught from there. In Uttar Pradesh, 10 to 20 day camps raised language scores by 0.70
standard deviations, a very large gain; in Haryana one hour a day in school gave 0.15 (Banerjee and others 2016).
This is the best Indian evidence, and CubeGullak already works this way: the learner's level, not age, decides the step.

**Duolingo.** What it does, and how strong the proof is:

| Mechanic | What it is | Evidence |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Spaced review | A word comes back just before it would be forgotten | Strong. Spacing and self-testing are among the best-proven ideas in learning research. Duolingo's own model predicted forgetting with about half the error of fixed schedules (Settles and Meeder 2016) |
| Right level | Each exercise is picked so the learner mostly gets it right but has to try | Good in principle (same idea as Pratham). Duolingo's numbers are its own and unpublished |
| Small lessons, instant feedback | A few minutes, one answer at a time | Good. Feedback at once is well proven |
| Streaks, XP, leagues | Reasons to come back daily | Weak. They bring people back; proof that they teach more is only Duolingo's own |

Duolingo's studies showing it teaches as well as college classes are mostly run by Duolingo. And it teaches a second
language to people who can already read. It has never had to teach a child to read a first script. So we take its
loop, not its content.

## 2. Does this fit "no reading in stages 1 and 2"?

Yes. Research asks for exactly this: spoken words and sound games first, with no print. The rule stays. Two things
make it safe:

- Stages are not years. A child ready for letters can clear houses 1 and 2 in days through the entry test.
- Houses 1 and 2 must not be empty waiting. They train the ear, which is what makes letters easy later.

## 3. The ring inside शब्द

sumeet, 2026-11-28: tapping शब्द opens another ring of circles, walked in order. At the last one the learner is a
reader and a writer. There are 12 houses: houses 1 to 11 are the Word part of Gyan's 11 stages, and house 12 is the
writer.

| House | Name | The learner can | The game |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | नाम Names | Name the things around them; follow a spoken sentence | Hear a word, tap its picture. Hear "the bird is on the tree", tap the right scene |
| 2 | ध्वनि Sounds | Break a word into beats; hear the first sound; hear a rhyme; follow a short tale | Tap once for each beat of पा-नी. Which picture starts like मछली? Which one does not rhyme? Hear a tale, answer by picture |
| 3 | अक्षर Letters | Read and write every akshara, vowel sign and joined letter; read short sentences; write their name | See section 4 |
| 4 | वाक्य Sentences | Read a one-page story smoothly and answer questions | Read, then tap the answer. Put jumbled words in order. Fill the missing word. Type a sentence |
| 5 | कहानी Stories | Read stories and poems; use gender, number, tense; write a letter home | Story with questions. Fix the wrong word. Finish the poem's line |
| 6 | जानकारी Facts | Read an information text and find the main idea; idioms | Pick the best title. Match idiom to meaning. Put the steps in order |
| 7 | निबंध Essay | Write a formal letter and a short essay | Arrange the parts of a letter. Choose the better sentence of two. Write to a prompt |
| 8 | रचना Word-building | See how words are built; read an unseen passage; diary and dialogue | Build words from root and ending. Guess a new word from its parts |
| 9 | अलंकार Style | Spot figures of speech; write a notice and a report | Find the simile. Rewrite plainly, then richly |
| 10 | पाठ Close reading | Read closely; argue in an essay; summarise | What does the writer mean here? Cut a passage to a third |
| 11 | विचार Ideas | Read articles and speeches; fill official papers | Fact or opinion? Find the weak point in an argument |
| 12 | लेखक Writer | Read whole works of literature in their language; write their own story, poem and essay | Read a classic in parts, with a few questions. Write; nothing is marked or shared |

What is read at each house comes from that age of the Gyan path (tales, fables, Jataka, Kalidasa, Kabir, Premchand),
so the houses lead into literature step by step.

## 4. House 3 in detail: how letters are taught

This is where the current game is wrong, and where most Indian children get stuck.

1. **A few aksharas at a time, most useful first.** Not the varnamala order. For Hindi, start with a set like म, न,
   क, र, ल and the sign ा. Each language needs its own order.
2. **Sound first.** Hear the sound, tap the akshara. See the akshara, tap the picture that starts with it.
3. **Join at once.** With 5 aksharas and one sign the child already reads नाम, काम, कल, नल, कमल. Every word is made
   only of what has been taught.
4. **Vowel signs as a grid** (barakhadi): क का कि की कु कू के कै को कौ. Hear one, tap it.
5. **Trace it.** Draw the akshara with a finger over a guide. Writing fixes the shape in memory.
6. **Joined letters last.**
7. **Then sentences and a short paragraph**, still made of known aksharas.

The wrong choices in a question differ by one akshara or one sign (काम, कम, नाम), so the child must read, not guess.

## 5. The game loop, for every house

- **A lesson is about 10 questions, 2 to 3 minutes.** One thing on screen, one tap, feedback at once with the sound.
- **New and old are mixed.** About 3 new items and 7 from before.
- **Missed items come back**: later in the same lesson, the next day, then after 3, 7 and 21 days. Kept on the phone
  (five boxes), so it costs the server nothing.
- **Right level.** If a learner gets under 6 of 10, the next lesson is easier; 9 or more, harder or skip ahead.
- **Progress is the ring filling**, house by house. No marks, ranks or leagues (Gyan's rule; the proof for them is weak).
- **A soft daily habit.** A small mark for each day learned. Missing a day costs nothing and is never scolded.
- **A lesson ends on something read or heard for joy**: a line of a rhyme, a tiny tale.

Limits to be honest about: the phone cannot yet hear a child read aloud or speak, so fluency and speech are practised
but not checked. Checking by voice in 11 languages is a later piece of work.

## 6. What to change in the current word game first

The game today: a picture, a speaker button, three written words; 12 words in a fixed loop; a second page of letters
in varnamala order spoken by the phone's voice.

1. **Flip it for beginners.** Hear the word, tap one of three pictures. No print. This is house 1.
2. **Move "pick the written word" to house 3**, after the aksharas in the word are taught, and with look-alike wrong
   choices. Today a child can pass by remembering shapes.
3. **Replace the letters page** with the house 3 lessons: sounds, not letter names; useful order; vowel signs.
4. **Choose first words per language** that are short and plain. Today's Hindi list has चिड़िया, कुत्ता, बिल्ली, which
   have joined letters and are among the hardest to read.
5. **Lessons of 10 with missed items returning**, in place of the fixed loop of 12.
6. **Recorded voice for every sound and word** (Sarvam, as already decided). The phone's voice says letters badly.

Steps 1 and 5 are small and can ship first.
