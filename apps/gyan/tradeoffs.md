# Gyan: tradeoffs and choices

Choices made without asking, 2026-11-28. Only the current choice is kept. Lines with `Human:` were decided by sumeet.

Format: `- [where] Choice. Why. Cost.`

- [scope] Human: the path has 12 stages and ends at class 11 level, good enough for JEE and NEET (2026-11-28; before that 11 stages to class 10). Why: sumeet dropped the number 11 for 12 everywhere, and said class 12 is mostly test preparation. Cost: the path is much heavier in science and maths.
- [scope] The class 12 science and maths that JEE and NEET ask are in the path too, marked 12. Why: the official JEE Main and NEET syllabus is NCERT class 11 and 12 together, so class 11 alone would not make a learner ready. Cost: more than sumeet's "up to class 11"; stages 8 to 11 become very heavy.
- [scope] Only science and maths go beyond class 10; history, geography, civics and economics stay at class 10 level. Why: JEE and NEET are the stated goal, and class 11 splits into streams. Cost: no class 11 humanities or commerce.
- [order] Human: everything is taught in the order people first learned it, not in NCERT's order. Why: it is the story of human growth, and a learner can walk it in a year. Cost: zero arrives at stage 6, so school arithmetic comes after the right triangle; Claude advised ordering maths by what it needs, and sumeet chose time.
- [view] Human: one world timeline seen from India (2026-11-28). An idea enters when people first found it anywhere; where India was first, India's story and name are used (Baudhayana's rule about 800 BCE before Pythagoras; Brahmagupta's zero in 628 CE). Why: the story stays linear and true, and India's own order also puts the triangle before zero. Cost: where the first finder is disputed we must pick one, and each such claim needs a source.
- [one path] Human: there are no separate subjects; it is one path. Why: "these are not separate, it is one thing". Cost: a learner cannot study only one side.
- [arts] Human: drawing, music and exercise (with martial arts and dance inside it) are part of the path; photography and performing arts are taught; nothing is recorded or shared. Why: these are learnt things too, and recording would make a social network. Cost: the app cannot see what the learner did.
- [name] Human: the tab is Gyan; Khabri becomes Vartaman and the horoscope becomes Bhavishya. Why: knowledge, the present, the future. Cost: this replaces the earlier rule that the news is called Khabri everywhere.
- [stages] The 12 stages are ages of history: before 10,000 BCE, to 3500 BCE, to 1500 BCE, to 300 BCE, to 600 CE, to 1200, to 1600, to 1750, to 1850, to 1900, to 1947, to today. Why: each cut is a turn in what people knew; the twelfth came from splitting 1850 to 1947 at 1900, where classical science ends and the atom and the quantum begin. Cost: the stages are unequal; 8 to 11 carry most of the JEE and NEET work.
- [unlock] Vartaman opens when stage 7 (to 1600) is cleared. Why: sumeet's mark is "finished class 6, knows the world roughly"; by 1600 the whole Earth is mapped and the learner reads at class 6 level. Cost: the match to class 6 is a judgement, since the path no longer follows classes.
- [labels] Each step has a label: Number, Nature, People, Earth, Rule, Money, Word, Art, Music, Body, Hand. Why: to check that the path holds everything. Cost: labels may look like subjects; they should stay small on screen.
- [hand] A Hand label was added: knots, pots, weaving, cooking, casting, paper, typing. Why: making things is part of human growth; sumeet named pottery himself. Cost: more do steps that cannot be checked.
- [do steps] A do step is shown, never tested; the learner taps done. Why: sumeet said to show and assume the learner does it. Cost: a learner can tap done without doing.
- [reading] Human: reading does not start on day one (2026-11-28); sumeet said to use judgement on the rest. Stages 1 and 2 have no reading: each step is a picture and a spoken word. Letters are taught in stage 3, where writing was invented, and steps are read from stage 4. Why: people spoke and drew long before they wrote, and it suits a learner who cannot read. Cost: every step of stages 1 and 2 needs a picture and a voice; stage 3 carries all of class 1 and 2 reading and is the heaviest stage for a new reader.
- [reading] The pigeon test (pick the written word) is the stage 3 test. In stages 1 and 2 the test is "hear the word, tap the picture". Why: nothing is read before stage 3. Cost: sumeet first described the written-word test as the very first one.
- [reading] English is taught to everyone as a second language from stage 4, one stage after the learner's own letters; a learner in English gets Hindi. Why: English is needed for forms and work, and two scripts at once is too much. Cost: English is not in order of time (it reached India in stage 8); done under sumeet's "use your judgement".
- [tests] The entry test is a skip check with 3 rounds of 5 questions, 4 right each; all passed clears the stage. Why: sumeet said about three tests are enough. Cost: 15 questions is a thin check of a whole age.
- [tests] The exit test is 10 mixed questions and 7 right passes. Why: sumeet said a learner weak in one thing should still move ahead; with a mix, one weak side costs only two or three questions. Cost: a learner can finish Gyan with a real gap, for example in maths. Done under sumeet's "use your judgement"; before the one-path decision he asked for separate progress per subject.
- [tests] Placement climbs from stage 1. Why: simplest. Cost: a well-read person answers about 150 questions; a faster way is in `backlog.md`.
- [tests] No marks or ranks are shown. Why: Kids have no scores in BodhiLabs. Cost: a learner cannot see how close they were.
- [numbers] Before stage 6, numbers are written as counts of ones, tens and hundreds, and sums use counters. Why: zero and written arithmetic had not come. Cost: this needs its own design and may confuse a learner who already knows digits.
- [history] India is told in full and the world beside it only where it turned the story. Why: NCERT does the same. Cost: China, Africa and the Americas are thin.
- [dates] Dates are the commonly given ones and unchecked. Why: this map is about order. Cost: each needs a check when its step is written.
- [ncert] Class 9 steps follow the earlier NCERT books because the 2026-27 chapter lists could not be read in full. Why: the topics are mostly the same. Cost: the class column may be off by one in places.
- [shabd] Human: शब्द opens its own ring of circles walked in order, ending in a reader and writer (2026-11-28). Claude chose 12 houses: 1 to 11 are the Word part of the 11 stages and 12 is the writer. Why: sumeet said "11 or 12", and the 12th gives literature and own writing a home. Cost: house 3 (letters) is far heavier than the others. Detail in `docs/shabd.md`.
- [shabd] Letters are taught a few at a time in order of use, by sound, and joined into words at once; the picture-to-written-word game moves to house 3. Why: reading research and the akshara studies. Cost: a letter order and word list must be made for each of the 11 languages.
- [shabd] No XP, leagues or lost streaks; only a soft mark for each day learned. Why: Gyan shows no marks or ranks, and the proof that these teach more is Duolingo's own. Cost: less pull to return daily.
- [lessons] Human: a lesson is not a video file; it is an animation the phone runs from code (2026-11-28). Claude chose plain SVG drawings moved by CSS, with no animation library, and one Sarvam recording per line. Why: Lottie and Rive need a designer's tool to make each file and add a player of 60 to 250 KB, and GSAP adds about 70 KB for moves CSS already does; a lesson made this way is a few KB, is written as code like the rest of the app, and one drawing serves all 12 languages. AI video (Veo, Sora, Kling) was ruled out: heavy files, a cost per second, and a new render per language. Cost: the drawings are simple, flat shapes and the phone's emoji; a richer hand-drawn scene would need a designer, and a Lottie file can be slotted into a scene later if wanted.
- [lessons] Animals, fruit and small things in a lesson are the phone's own emoji; fire, people, land and sky are drawn. Why: emoji weigh nothing and the app already uses them as pictures. Cost: they look a little different on each phone, and stone-age people are plain figures.
- [lessons] A lesson is heard once, in one voice (a man's or a woman's, picked when it opens), not twice like a single word. Why: a whole lesson said twice is slow; the same choice as a life read aloud. Cost: differs from the "everything is heard twice" rule for words.
- [lessons] A scene moves on when its line has been said; there are no time marks inside a line. Why: it stays in step in every language without timing each recording. Cost: a move cannot hit an exact word.
- [lessons] First three lessons are Nature, stage 1 (fire, five senses, animals), with lines in Hindi and English; other languages hear English until the lines are approved and written. Why: sumeet reviews one small batch first. Cost: ten languages wait.
- [lessons] Human: each stage must hold a full school year of material, as good as a parallel school, so that no other book is needed; a learner may go faster or mark a thing done and skip (2026-11-28). Claude sized one subject in one stage at about 144 short lessons: 12 chapters of 12, each lesson one to two minutes with a hear-and-tap check, so a stage is about 1,700 lessons. Why: a school year is about 36 weeks, and three or four lessons a week for a subject gives 110 to 140; 12 by 12 fits the app's grid. Cost: the count is an estimate, not taken from a syllabus document; stage 1 Nature has 7 lessons built so far.
- [lessons] Human: every chapter is told by a great person of its field, in the first person and in their manner, never as an AI (2026-11-28). Claude's choices for stage 1 Nature: body Sushruta, senses Charaka, fire Faraday, animals Aristotle, birds Salim Ali, plants J. C. Bose, food Janaki Ammal; the teller opens by saying who they are, with one true thing from their life; the Sarvam voice matches the teller (a man's or a woman's). Why: these people are known for exactly these things. Cost: the words are written in their manner and are not their own quotes; a little child will not know the names; only one of seven tellers is a woman.
- [strands] Human: Ayurveda, jyotish and astronomy, world religions, geography, yoga and breath, Indian music and dance, vastu and architecture, farming and cooking, money, law and rights, logic and debate, coding and machines, first aid, Sanskrit and word roots, crafts and ecology run through all 12 stages as strands inside the existing labels, not as new houses (2026-11-28). Why: they are worth knowing and school teaches them poorly. Cost: every stage grows; the map gives them in four bands of three stages, not yet step by step.
- [strands] Jyotish, vastu and Ayurveda are taught as traditions and astronomy, building science and medicine as science, with each step saying which it is; Ayurveda is never offered in place of a doctor. Why: sumeet asked for the two to be clearly separated, and health advice can hurt. Cost: some will find the line too cautious, others not cautious enough.
- [strands] Religions are told each in its own words, in the order they arose, with no comparison or ranking. Why: respect, and it is the safest way to teach faith to children of every family. Cost: hard questions between religions are left out; every such step needs a reader from that faith before it ships.
- [ask] Human: the "?" button answers from our own material, on the phone, with a small language model over it (RAG), first for stage 1 and later for all twelve stages (2026-11-28). Built in two halves: finding the lines (`lib/find.js`), and a small model that rewords them (`lib/mind.js`).
- [ask] Finding is plain word matching (BM25 over words and three-letter pieces, word forms joined by their beginnings), not an embedding model. Why: it needs no download, works on every phone and in every language the lessons are written in, and answers in under 10 ms; the smallest embedding model that knows Indian languages (multilingual-e5-small) is 118 MB. Cost: it matches words, not meaning, so "what does fire give" can find the wrong line about fire; an embedding model is the next step if this shows in real questions (`ask:near` and `ask:none` counts, and the questions reaching admin).
- [ask] The answer is the lesson's own line, with its drawing and its recorded Sarvam voice in the teller's voice. Why: stage 1 and 2 learners cannot read, and a recorded line is always correct and sounds right in every language. Cost: the line may not fit the exact question.
- [ask] An answer is "sure" when 60% of the question's telling words are in the line; otherwise the nearest line is shown and the question also goes to a person; with no match it only goes to a person. Why: a wrong answer to a child is worse than a late one. Cost: uses the five-a-day limit; the two stop-word lists (Hindi, English) are hand-written until the lessons are many enough for counting to do it.
- [ask] A question can be spoken: the browser's own speech recognition writes it down. Why: no cost, no code, and little children cannot type. Cost: on Chrome the sound goes to Google to be written down, so this one step is not on the phone; iPhone and some browsers have none and show only the box. Sarvam's speech-to-text through our Worker is the other way, at a price per minute.
- [ask] The model is Gemma 3 270M (273 MB, q4f16) run by Transformers.js 4.3.2 on WebGPU, loaded from jsDelivr and Hugging Face. Why: the smallest current model with a ready browser build; Qwen 0.5B and Gemma 1B are two to three times the download. Cost: not tried on any real phone (the build machine cannot reach the model files); its Hindi is unknown and likely weak; its words have no recording, so the phone's own voice reads them; two outside hosts, which goes against "Cloudflare only" until the files are moved to R2.
- [ask] The model is off for learners. It is tried at `/lab`, and a switch there turns it on in Ask for that one phone. Why: untested, and 273 MB on mobile data is real money for a family. Cost: step 2 reaches nobody until sumeet has tried it and says when to download.
- [ask] Only lessons are searched, in the learner's language, else English. Lives are not searched yet (they have one recording for the whole text, not per line). At scale the lines are loaded per stage (about 100 bytes a line); `passages(lang, { upTo })` already leaves out stages above the learner's.
- [ask] Human: the phone's model comes in quietly in the background; until it is there, and on weak phones, a cheap model on Cloudflare writes the answer from the same lines; then the phone takes over (2026-11-28, after choosing "only after I test /lab" for the phone's model). So the cloud answer is on for everyone now, and the phone's model still waits for the /lab trial.
- [ask] The cloud model is Workers AI `@cf/google/gemma-3-12b-it`. Why: Gemma is the open family that writes Indian languages best at this size; one answer is about 11 neurons, a hundredth of a rupee, and 10,000 neurons a day are free (about 900 answers). Cost: not yet seen answering on the live site; llama-3.2-3b is a seventh of the price but weaker in Hindi and marked for removal.
- [ask] The phone sends only the names of the lines ("fire.4"); the Worker reads their words from the lessons. Why: nothing but our own material can be put before the model, whatever a phone sends. Cost: the question itself is still free text, and it does leave the phone on this path.
- [ask] At most 20 newly written cloud answers per phone a day, and each answer is kept (in the VOICE KV, under `answer/`) by question words and lines, so the same question is written once for everyone. Why: a low, known ceiling on cost (20 paise per phone a day at the very most). Cost: past the cap the learner hears the found line instead; kept answers share the voice store until they get their own.
- [ask] The model's sentence is read by the phone's own voice; the found line keeps its recorded Sarvam voice (a press on the drawing). Why: a new sentence has no recording, and making one each time costs money and seconds. Cost: the phone's voice is poorer, and missing for some languages.
- [ask] A phone gets the model by itself only when: WebGPU gives an adapter, memory is 4 GB or more, the chip takes a 256 MB block, 1 GB of room is free, data saver is off, and it says it is on Wi-Fi or cable (`power`, `mayFetch` in `lib/mind.js`; shown at /lab). Why: a browser cannot measure speed directly, these are what it tells. Cost: Chrome rounds memory to at most 8 GB; iPhone tells neither memory nor Wi-Fi, so an iPhone never downloads by itself; the download runs on the page's own thread and may make it stutter until moved to a worker.
