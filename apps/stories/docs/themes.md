# Stories: the themes (draft for sumeet's yes or no)

Written 2026-11-28. Themes only; no story is written yet. 12 stages x 12 themes = 144. A stage's stories open when the
learner reaches that stage. Each theme uses something the learner meets in that stage of Gyan.

Each theme gives: the premise, the **Idea** (the piece of logic and the value it teaches), and the **Twist**.
Mark each one yes or no; a no gets replaced.

---

## Stage 1: the first people (pre-school; heard with pictures, about 2 minutes)

**1.1 The Shadow That Would Not Go Home**
Mitti is scared of the dark shape that follows her. She runs, hides and even tries to give it away, but it stays.
- Idea: light makes the shadow. What you understand, you stop fearing.
- Twist: at night the shadow is gone and she misses it. Grandmother's fire brings it back as a giant, and Mitti learns to make it dance with her hands.

**1.2 You Cut, I Choose**
Two sisters find one sweet fruit. Whoever cuts it, the other cries "yours is bigger!"
- Idea: fairness can be built into the rule: one cuts, the other chooses first.
- Twist: the cutter now cuts so carefully that the chooser cannot find a bigger half, and both start laughing.

**1.3 The Bird With No Name**
A new bird comes to the camp. Everyone calls it by a different name and it comes to no one.
- Idea: listen before you speak. A name works only when both sides agree on it.
- Twist: the child who sits quietly hears the bird say its own name, and finds the bird already had a call for her.

**1.4 One Pebble for One Goat**
Little Bholu cannot count past three, but he must bring all the goats home. He drops one pebble in a bag for each goat that goes out.
- Idea: matching one to one. Care means no one is left out.
- Twist: at dusk one pebble is left over, so he searches and finds the lost goat. Then he is one pebble short: she has had a kid.

**1.5 The Fire That Was Always Hungry**
The children are given a small fire to look after. It crackles for more, and they keep feeding it.
- Idea: enough and too much. A fire eats only what it is given.
- Twist: the fire grows frightening and the big children run. The smallest child simply stops feeding it and it becomes small and friendly again.

**1.6 Who Took the Night?**
The owl chick thinks the Sun steals the night. The rooster chick thinks the night steals the day. Each stays awake to catch the thief.
- Idea: day and night take turns. Taking turns is not stealing.
- Twist: they meet at dawn, the one moment both are awake, and agree to share the swing the same way.

**1.7 The Hand on the Cave Wall**
A wall is covered with handprints. The child tries each one: too big, too big, too big.
- Idea: comparing sizes; everything grows and changes. Belonging to a family.
- Twist: one tiny print fits no one in the band. It is her own, made when she was a baby, and now her hand is bigger than it.

**1.8 The Sweet That Looked Like a Stone**
A child refuses the ugly brown lump and picks the shiny red berry. The berry is bitter and the lump is sweet.
- Idea: do not judge by one sense. Look, smell, touch, then decide.
- Twist: grandmother, who cannot see, picks the sweet one every time, so she becomes the teacher of the game.

**1.9 The Longest Tail**
The animals argue about who is the biggest. Each one measures in the way that suits itself.
- Idea: to measure, everyone must use the same way. And "biggest" means many things: each one is simply itself.
- Twist: the snake is the longest, the giraffe the tallest, the elephant the heaviest. Then the ant climbs on the elephant's head, and together they reach a fruit that no one could reach alone.

**1.10 The Clap That Crossed the River**
Fog comes and a child is cut off from the band by the river. She cannot see anyone, so she claps the family beat.
- Idea: a pattern is a message. Stay calm and use what you know.
- Twist: two answers come back. One only copies her (the echo off the rock). The other finishes the beat. She follows the one that finishes it.

**1.11 The Mountain You Could Put in Your Pocket**
From the camp the far mountain looks smaller than a pebble. "I can jump over it," says the child, and sets off.
- Idea: far things look small. How big a thing looks depends on where you stand.
- Twist: the mountain grows with every step. She turns round and her whole home has become a dot she could put in her pocket.

**1.12 The Footprints Told**
The honey is gone. "The bear took it," says a sticky little boy. The mud by the tree is full of footprints.
- Idea: what we do leaves marks. The truth is easier to carry than a made-up story.
- Twist: the prints are small with five toes: his. He owns up. Beside them are a cub's prints too, so when he later shouts "bear!", everyone believes him.

## Stage 2: farms and villages (class 1; heard with pictures, about 3 minutes)

**2.1 The Seed That Was Told to Hurry**
A child plants a seed and digs it up every morning to see if it has grown.
- Idea: some things cannot be hurried, and checking can itself do harm. Patience.
- Twist: the seed she dropped on the path and forgot is the first one to sprout.

**2.2 The Pot That Could Not Go Back**
A child bakes her crooked pot in secret, then wants to squash it and start again. Baked clay will not become soft again.
- Idea: some changes cannot be undone, so think first. Then make the best of what is done.
- Twist: the crooked lip pours water without spilling a drop. Soon every potter in the village is copying her "mistake".

**2.3 The Square Wheel**
The village's first cart has square wheels and goes thump, thump. The children try three corners (worse), then six, then twelve.
- Idea: improve step by step and watch what each step does.
- Twist: every extra corner makes the ride smoother. So the smallest child asks how many corners the smoothest wheel has: none, or more than anyone can count?

**2.4 The Flute That Cost a Song**
A girl wants a flute. The flute maker wants honey, the honey man wants a rope, the rope woman wants a pot, and so on down the lane.
- Idea: a chain of swaps; every link must keep its word.
- Twist: the last person in the chain wants only a tune played on a flute. The circle closes on the girl herself.

**2.5 The Missing Bead**
Grandmother's necklace breaks: red, red, blue, red, red, blue. The children thread it again but something looks wrong.
- Idea: a pattern tells you what is missing and where. Owning up.
- Twist: the pattern shows two beads are gone, not one. One is in a little brother's fist. The other is in a magpie's nest, along with everything the village has lost this year.

**2.6 Ten Fingers, Eleven Sheep**
A shepherd counts on his fingers and has one sheep too many for his hands. He puts down one stone to mean "a full pair of hands".
- Idea: grouping in tens. One mark can stand for many.
- Twist: a stranger who counts on fingers and toes gets a different-looking count for the same flock. Both are right, and they trade ways of counting.

**2.7 The Frog Who Was Wrong Once**
The village must choose the day to sow. Everyone trusts the frogs' song, but a child has been watching the ants too.
- Idea: a sign is not a cause. Watch carefully, and speak up even if you are small.
- Twist: her ant sign fails once as well. She learns to trust two signs together more than either alone.

**2.8 The Mouse Who Guarded the Seed**
In the granary, a greedy mouse wants to eat the seed grain now. His careful cousin hides a little.
- Idea: one grain kept gives many grains later. Think of tomorrow.
- Twist: the hidden grain sprouts into a patch that feeds them all. The greedy mouse is made guard of the seed, because he knows the temptation best.

**2.9 The Drum Message**
A message goes from village to village by drum and by mouth: "bring the goats". At each stop it changes a little.
- Idea: passing something on exactly is hard. Check by sending it back.
- Twist: it arrives as "bring the boats". The puzzled villagers bring boats, the river rises that night, and afterwards they invent the rule of repeating a message back.

**2.10 The Pup Who Chose**
Two children want the same wild pup. One ties it with a rope. The other puts out food each day and leaves the door open.
- Idea: trust is built by many small acts. Friendship cannot be forced.
- Twist: the pup bites through the rope and runs away. Next morning it is asleep in the open doorway.

**2.11 The Field With No Owner**
The grazing field belongs to everyone. Each family adds "just one more goat", and soon there is no grass at all.
- Idea: if everyone takes a little more, everyone loses. What is shared needs a shared rule.
- Twist: the children mark the field in parts and take turns. The rested patch grows back and, under it, they find a spring.

**2.12 Who Is Eating the Moon?**
A girl is sure someone takes a bite of the Moon each night. She ties one knot in a string for every night to catch the thief.
- Idea: a pattern that repeats can be counted, and then foretold. Watching patiently.
- Twist: the Moon grows back, and her knots repeat. She names the next full-moon night in advance, and the village fixes its harvest feast by her string.

## Stage 3: the first cities (class 2; the learner's first read stories, half a page)

**3.1 The Crack in the Seal**
Every trader presses a seal on their goods. Someone has copied the bead maker's seal and is selling bad beads under her name.
- Idea: a mark is useful only if it is hard to copy. Honest trade.
- Twist: her real seal has a tiny crack that prints every time. The flaw she was ashamed of is the proof that saves her name.

**3.2 The Unknown Heap**
A merchant sets the children a riddle: "A heap and a quarter of the heap make fifteen. How big is the heap?"
- Idea: guess, check and correct. A wrong guess is a tool, not a shame.
- Twist: the child who guesses "four" gets five, sees it is three times too small, and jumps straight to twelve. The wrong answer was the quickest road.

**3.3 The Shopkeeper With Two Sets of Weights**
A shopkeeper buys with heavy weights and sells with light ones. A girl has a balance but no weights of her own.
- Idea: test a thing against itself: swap the sides and see. Fair measure.
- Twist: she buys grain from him and sells the same grain straight back. He loses to his own weights and throws the false set in the river.

**3.4 The Girl Who Wrote Herself a Door**
In the Scribes' City, whatever is written on clay comes true, exactly as written. A girl writes "no more rain" and "I want to be the tallest".
- Idea: say exactly what you mean. Words have results.
- Twist: baked clay cannot be rubbed out. She has to undo each wish by writing a cleverer sentence.

**3.5 Half of a Half of a Half**
A giant is promised half the sweets today, half of what is left tomorrow, and so on. He is afraid they will run out.
- Idea: halving never quite reaches nothing. A sharer always has something left.
- Twist: the pieces get so small that only the ants can carry them. The giant, who was always given half, begins to give half.

**3.6 The Shadow Clock Thief**
The city tells time by a shadow stick. Someone keeps tilting it so that the market closes early.
- Idea: check a claim against another witness: her own shadow does not agree with the stick. Then ask why.
- Twist: the "thief" is a tired water boy who only wanted the day to end. The city keeps the true stick and adds a rest at noon.

**3.7 The Bridge Law**
The law on the wall says "No one may cross the bridge after dark." One night a healer must cross to reach a sick child.
- Idea: a rule has a purpose, and the purpose matters more than the words.
- Twist: the guard thinks hard and carries her across: "She did not cross. I did, and I am the guard." In the morning the city writes the law better.

**3.8 The Buffalo on the Road**
A buffalo is asleep on the only road. The strongest man in the city cannot move it.
- Idea: the lever. A small push in the right place beats a big push in the wrong one.
- Twist: a girl tips it awake with a long pole and a stone. Then it will not walk, until she shows it sweet grass. Mind first, then kindness.

**3.9 The Map With a Hole in It**
Children find a clay map with one piece broken off, right where the mark for treasure should be.
- Idea: work out the missing part from what is known: a river must go on, a road must lead somewhere.
- Twist: the path they reason out ends where they started. The map is of their own city long ago and the "treasure" is the old well under their courtyard.

**3.10 The Debt and the Seed**
A girl borrows one bowl of grain and promises two bowls back, doubling for every moon she is late. She is late three times.
- Idea: doubling grows very fast. Promise with care.
- Twist: she had planted the borrowed bowl. Grain doubles faster than the debt, and she pays in full with seed to spare.

**3.11 Copper, Tin and the Bell**
Soft Copper and weak Tin are laughed at by hard Stone. The smith melts the two together.
- Idea: a whole can have a strength that neither part has. Friendship.
- Twist: as bronze they ring, a note neither could make alone. That bell later wakes the city when the river floods.

**3.12 The Broken Pot Detectives**
Children digging by the old wall find broken pots, burnt grain and a row of post holes. Who lived here, and what happened?
- Idea: reasoning from remains to what must have been. Respect for those who came before.
- Twist: the last find is a tiny clay cart: a child lived here. They bury a toy of their own for whoever digs next.

## Stage 4: iron and ideas (class 3; one page)

**4.1 The Rope With Twelve Knots**
A builder's boy learns that a rope knotted 3, 4 and 5 always gives a true corner. He tests every wall he can find.
- Idea: a true rule works every time and anyone can check it. Do the work right even where nobody looks.
- Twist: the master's own famous wall fails the rope. The master thanks the boy in front of everyone.

**4.2 The Tree Too Tall to Climb**
The children want to hang a festival lamp from the top of the village's oldest tree. How long a rope do they need? Nobody can climb that high safely.
- Idea: measure by shadow: when your shadow is as long as you, the tree's shadow is as long as the tree. Thinking instead of risking.
- Twist: the day turns cloudy and there are no shadows. A girl uses a stick and the tree's reflection in a puddle. The rope is cut once and reaches exactly.

**4.3 The Odd One at the Dance**
In the Land of Pairs everything goes two by two. An "odd" child is always the one left over.
- Idea: odd and even. Two odds together make an even.
- Twist: she finds the leftover child of the next village. Then they invent a circle dance in which nobody needs a pair at all.

**4.4 Seven Laddoos**
Seven laddoos, and they will not share equally among two friends, or three, or four, or five, or six.
- Idea: prime numbers. Some things divide only one way.
- Twist: the only fair way is to call friends until there are seven. The stubborn number makes the party bigger.

**4.5 The Line Nobody Could Name**
A tile maker measures the corner-to-corner line of a square tile. No fraction fits it, however fine. The scholars say he measures badly.
- Idea: some true things do not fit the boxes we have. It is honest to say "I cannot name it yet".
- Twist: his daughter says, "I cannot say it, but I can draw it," and draws it. It exists whether or not it has a name.

**4.6 The Ant Road**
A prince's chariot is about to cross a long line of ants. A child stands in the way. The court logician says, "Small things do not matter."
- Idea: test a rule by turning it round: would you accept it if you were the small one?
- Twist: "Then I do not matter to you, and you do not matter to the mountain," says the child. The prince gets down and walks round.

**4.7 The Stone That Chose North**
A girl finds a stone that pulls iron. Half the village wants to worship it and half wants to throw it away.
- Idea: test a wonder: what does it pull, what not, through what? Neither fear nor blind belief.
- Twist: a man selling "wishing stones" is doing the same trick with a hidden piece. The children then use theirs to find every lost needle in the village.

**4.8 The Loudest Voice**
The assembly decides by who shouts loudest. A quiet girl knows where the new well should be dug.
- Idea: loud is not the same as right. One person, one pebble.
- Twist: they count pebbles, and she still loses. So she asks only for a small test hole. The water decides, and the assembly changes its mind.

**4.9 The Girl Who Could Not Run Out of Sentences**
The Silence King tells a talkative girl that there are only so many sentences and she has nearly used hers up.
- Idea: a few rules of grammar make endless new sentences. Speech is an endless gift, so spend it on true and kind words.
- Twist: he demands a sentence never spoken before. She adds "and" to his own sentence, again and again, until he laughs and gives up.

**4.10 Long, Short, Long**
A friend is ill and must stay alone in his house. Two children make a code of long and short knocks on the wall.
- Idea: counting arrangements: three knocks give eight different signals. Loyalty.
- Twist: the sick friend answers with a pattern they never agreed on. He has made a new word, and the code begins to grow.

**4.11 The Ship That Sank Without Sinking**
Children on the shore watch a ship leave. Its body disappears first, then its sail. They run crying that it has sunk.
- Idea: evidence that the Earth is a ball. Reason before you panic.
- Twist: an old sailor asks, "If it comes back, which part will you see first?" They guess the sail, wait, and are right.

**4.12 The Brother Who Could Not Be Caught**
A clever talker "proves" that a girl can never catch her little brother if he starts ahead: each time she reaches where he was, he has moved on.
- Idea: an argument can sound perfect and still be wrong. If you can test it, test it.
- Twist: she simply runs and catches him. Then she sits up all night to find the flaw: endless small steps can fit inside a short time.

## Stage 5: the great empires (class 4; one to two pages)

**5.1 The Iron Bowl That Would Not Drown**
Everyone knows iron sinks. A girl bets her kite that she can make iron float.
- Idea: the same stuff in a different shape behaves differently. "Everyone knows" is not a proof.
- Twist: when the river floods, the smith's heavy tools are ferried to safety in his own iron cooking pots.

**5.2 The Town Behind the Mirror**
A left-handed boy, teased as "wrong-handed", walks into a town where everything is reversed: writing, clocks, handshakes.
- Idea: a mirror image. No hand is the wrong one; it depends on which side you stand.
- Twist: only he can read the mirror town's signs and shake its hands. He becomes the go-between and both towns start to trade.

**5.3 The Debt Below Nothing**
"Nobody can have less than nothing," says the merchant. But his daughter's account shows that the family owes three goats.
- Idea: numbers below nothing are debts. Keep honest accounts.
- Twist: she draws who owes whom. The baker owes the potter, the potter owes the weaver, the weaver owes the baker. The debts cancel and a year-old quarrel ends without one coin moving.

**5.4 The Game Where Nobody Lost**
A prince plays chaturanga by throwing away his foot soldiers. An old soldier asks him to give each piece a name first.
- Idea: count the cost. A gain that costs more than it brings is a loss.
- Twist: a girl plays him without taking a single piece, and he cannot break through. Together they invent a way to play in which both sides finish the board as partners.

**5.5 Proof, Please**
In the town of Sabit, nothing is believed without proof. A smooth stranger "proves", step by step, that everyone owes him dinner.
- Idea: a proof is a chain, and one false link breaks it. Do not be carried by confident talk.
- Twist: the children accept his proof, then use the very same steps to show he owes them breakfast for a year. He points to the false link himself.

**5.6 The Well at Noon**
Two friends in far-apart towns write to each other. "At noon the Sun shines straight down my well." "Not in mine. You are joking."
- Idea: a small difference, measured honestly, can reveal something huge: the Earth curves, and you can find its size.
- Twist: each thought the other was lying. Trusting each other was the first step of the discovery.

**5.7 The Painter Who Stole the Roundness**
A painted ball looks so round that a prince tries to pick it up. He calls the painter a sorcerer.
- Idea: light and shade persuade the eye. A picture that says "I am a picture" is art; a trick used to cheat is a lie.
- Twist: the painter shows exactly how it is done. Later a painted doorway on the treasury wall stops two thieves, nose first.

**5.8 The Dancer With Nine Faces**
A child actor can show nine moods so well that the village thinks she is really furious or really heartbroken.
- Idea: feelings can be seen, named and put down again. You are not your mood.
- Twist: when two families quarrel, she plays each side's feeling to the other. They see themselves and begin to laugh.

**5.9 The Rope Fence**
The village gives a girl one rope to fence a nursery for seedlings, safe from the goats. She wants to fit in as many seedlings as she can.
- Idea: of all shapes made with the same rope, the circle holds the most. Then think again: what is already there?
- Twist: she tries a strip, a triangle and a square, counting seedlings each time, and reaches the circle. Then she lays the rope as a half circle against the river bank and has room left over for a playground.

**5.10 The Monster That Ate the Moon**
The Moon goes dark and the town bangs pots in terror. A man collects coins to "save" it.
- Idea: an eclipse is the Earth's shadow, and it can be predicted. Fear ends where understanding begins.
- Twist: a girl who has kept a sky diary names the night of the next one, free of charge. She also notices the shadow is always round.

**5.11 The Empty Place**
A clerk writes three hundred and two as "3  2" with a gap. The gap is missed, and a caravan carries food for thirty-two camels.
- Idea: a digit's place gives its value. Even "nothing" needs a mark.
- Twist: the stable boy, always told he is nothing, puts a dot in the gap. Nothing, in the right place, is worth a great deal.

**5.12 The Boy Who Could Not Sit Still**
A restless boy in a caravan is taught one game by an old traveller: sit, and count your own breaths to ten without losing count.
- Idea: you can watch your own mind as you watch a bird. A calm mind notices more.
- Twist: a dust storm hides the caravan. The others run and shout. He sits, breathes, and is the only one who hears the camel bells.

## Stage 6: zero and algebra (class 5; two pages)

**6.1 Shunya Comes to Number Street**
The digits live on a street in order of rank. A newcomer, Zero, is worth nothing and is given the last hut.
- Idea: zero's powers: adding it changes nothing, standing beside you it makes you ten times bigger, multiplying by it takes everything.
- Twist: proud Nine multiplies himself by Zero to get rich and vanishes. And when asked to share treasure "among no one", Zero refuses: the one thing even Zero will not do.

**6.2 The Stranger Called X**
A masked visitor at the fair says, "I am a number. Find me." The balance keeper gives one rule: whatever you do to one pan, do to the other.
- Idea: solving an equation is keeping a balance fair, step by step.
- Twist: the last riddle has two right answers. The mask comes off and X is a pair of twins.

**6.3 The Council in the Pot**
The village picks its council by drawing name leaves from a pot. A rich man slips in twenty leaves with his name.
- Idea: a fair chance for all, and rules everyone can watch. Office is not for sale.
- Twist: the child who draws notices twenty leaves in one handwriting. From then on the leaves are counted aloud in front of all. Next year his single leaf is drawn fairly, and he serves well.

**6.4 The Hole in the Shutter**
Two scholars have argued for years: do eyes send out rays, or does light come in? A girl darkens a room and makes one small hole.
- Idea: settle a question by experiment, not by who argues best.
- Twist: the street appears on the wall, upside down. In it she spots the goat everyone has been hunting for. Each scholar turns out to be half wrong.

**6.5 The Needle That Seemed to Lie**
In thick fog the captain's feeling says one way and the compass needle says another. The crew splits.
- Idea: trust a tested tool over a feeling, and know what can fool the tool.
- Twist: the needle really is off. The cabin girl finds the cook's iron pot beside it, moves the pot, and the needle swings true.

**6.6 The Dancer in the Mould**
To cast a bronze figure, the wax model must be melted away. The apprentice cannot bear to destroy her best work.
- Idea: some gains need a letting go that cannot be undone. An empty space can hold a shape.
- Twist: the ugly clay lump she nearly threw out is where the dancer truly lives. The beauty was kept by the hollow.

**6.7 The Paper Promise**
A merchant's daughter crosses the country carrying one written note instead of gold. Bandits take it and laugh at it.
- Idea: money is a kept promise. Paper has value only where there is trust.
- Twist: the bandits cannot turn it into a single coin, because the banker asks a question only the honest holder can answer. The youngest bandit returns it and is hired as a messenger.

**6.8 The Song and the Rain**
A boy is sure that his teacher's rain song brings rain. He decides to test it: thirty days, a mark for song, a mark for rain.
- Idea: two things happening together does not mean one causes the other. Be honest when a dear belief fails the test.
- Twist: the rain does not obey. But his marks show something else every single time: listeners grow calm, and a feud ends. That power is real.

**6.9 The Slow Drip**
A caravan is stuck with only salt water. A girl sets pots in the sun so that steam gathers and drips.
- Idea: a mixture can be separated by what its parts do. Patience.
- Twist: they get sweet water, and the "useless" salt left behind pays for the whole journey.

**6.10 The Empty Hand**
A small girl joins a fighting school because of a bully. For a year the teacher teaches her only how to fall and how to step aside.
- Idea: use the other's force. The strongest move is often the one not made.
- Twist: one day the bully charges at her. She steps aside, and then she catches his arm so that he does not go over the edge.

**6.11 The Town of Opposite Tuesdays**
In one town everyone must say the opposite of the truth on Tuesdays. A lost girl needs to know the way and what day it is.
- Idea: logic of true and false. A lie only works where truth is normal.
- Twist: she asks, "Is today Tuesday?" and realises everyone answers "No" on every day. So she stops asking and looks: the baker bakes sweet bread only on Tuesdays.

**6.12 The Curd Song**
Grandmother says curd sets only if you sing the curd song while stirring. A girl sets many pots, changing one thing in each.
- Idea: to find a cause, change one thing at a time. Test a custom with respect, not scorn.
- Twist: the words do not matter, but the song's length does: it times the stirring exactly. The custom was carrying real knowledge.

## Stage 7: the world is found (class 6; two to three pages)

**7.1 The Day That Vanished**
A ship sails right round the world. The cabin girl has marked every sunrise, yet at home her count is one day short. They call her careless.
- Idea: the date depends on which way you travelled. Two honest counts can differ with no liar.
- Twist: with a lamp and an orange she shows why, and correctly says what a ship sailing the other way would count.

**7.2 The Sum in the Sunflower**
A girl counts the spirals in sunflowers and pine cones: 8 and 13, 21 and 34. Each number is the sum of the two before it.
- Idea: one simple rule can build nature's patterns, and a pattern lets you predict.
- Twist: she is shown a "very old" carving of a sunflower. The spiral count is wrong, and that is how the forgery is caught.

**7.3 The King Who Lost in His Own Court**
The king signed the law himself: whoever's animal eats a crop must pay. His own horse eats a farmer's field.
- Idea: a rule that spares its maker is not a rule. The law is above the king.
- Twist: he tries to change the law mid-case, and finds his own law forbids it. He pays, and people begin to trust his word and his coins more than before.

**7.4 The Finger From Nowhere**
The people in a painting are perfectly flat. A child pokes a finger through their world: they see only a dot that grows into a circle and shrinks away.
- Idea: dimensions. What we see may be one slice of something larger.
- Twist: from above she can see inside their locked box and tells them where the lost key is. Then she wonders who can see inside hers.

**7.5 The Friend Who Ran Backwards**
On a round track, a girl passes a slower friend and sees her seem to slide backwards. Her grandmother has seen planets do the same.
- Idea: motion depends on where you watch from. The simpler explanation is that we are moving too.
- Twist: she can explain the sky. The harder problem is saying so without mocking those who think otherwise, and she finds a way: she lets them run the track.

**7.6 A Thousand Copies**
A scribe makes one book a year; the new press makes a thousand. Then one wrong letter is found in a printed recipe for medicine.
- Idea: a tool that multiplies truth multiplies mistakes equally. Check before you spread.
- Twist: the apprentice whose slip it was owns up, traces every buyer through the sales book, and invents the printed correction slip.

**7.7 The Weaver's Upside-Down Riddle**
A weaver sings riddles that sound like nonsense: "The blanket rains and the roof gets wet." Three groups in town each insist he belongs to them.
- Idea: look past the label to the meaning. A paradox is a door, not a wall.
- Twist: each group solves one line and no group can solve it alone. Put together, the answer is the same water in differently named pots.

**7.8 The Boy Who Was Called Dull**
A boy cannot read the board and is called slow. His grandmother cannot thread a needle. A lens grinder comes to town.
- Idea: the fault may be in the instrument, not the mind. Test before you judge a person.
- Twist: holding his lens and hers in a line, the boy pulls the far hills close. The "dull" boy is the first in town to see the Moon's mountains.

**7.9 The Book That Was Written Twice**
A trader writes every deal twice: once as what came in, once as what went out. One evening the two sides do not match and the new clerk is blamed.
- Idea: every give has a take, so a balance shows up any error. Honesty can be checked.
- Twist: the trader's daughter finds the slip is her own. Owning up clears the clerk, and he teaches her his trick for finding errors fast.

**7.10 Where Two Lines Cross**
Two captains who do not trust each other each know one number for an island: one knows how far north, the other how far east.
- Idea: one fact gives a line; two independent facts give a point. Cooperation needs honesty to work at all.
- Twist: each gives the other a slightly false number, and both dig up empty sand. Only the two true numbers cross on the island.

**7.11 The Answer That Never Ended**
An apprentice must fit an iron rim to a wheel. The old rule says "three times across". She measures, finds it a little short, and keeps adding small corrections.
- Idea: some truths are reached by endless steps, each one closer. Close enough to build with; never finished learning.
- Twist: a rim cut by the old "exact" rule does not fit. Her "unfinished" answer fits perfectly.

**7.12 Half Off, and Half Off Again**
A shop shouts, "Half off! And then half off again!" People rush in thinking the cloth is free.
- Idea: a percentage is always "of what?". Ten up and then ten down does not bring you back.
- Twist: a girl works out the true prices on the wall for everyone. The shopkeeper, a little ashamed, switches to plain prices, and his customers stay.

## Stage 8: the age of science (class 7; three pages)

**8.1 The Seven Bridges of Nadipur**
An old custom says: on the festival day, walk the town crossing each of its seven bridges exactly once. Nobody ever has. A girl's grandmother wishes to do it once, and a man sells route maps.
- Idea: you can prove a thing impossible by counting the bridges at each bank. Knowing when to stop trying is knowledge too.
- Twist: after weeks of trying, the girl proves no route can exist. The town builds an eighth bridge, and her grandmother makes the walk.

**8.2 The Feather and the Iron Ball**
"Heavy things fall faster," says the elder. The children climb the tower with an iron ball, a wooden ball and a feather.
- Idea: test the obvious. And when it fails, look for what made it seem true.
- Twist: the two balls land together, but the feather really is slower. Flat paper and crumpled paper show why: air. The elder was not a fool.

**8.3 The Coin That Remembered**
A fairground man says, "Five heads in a row! Tails is due. Bet now." The children keep losing.
- Idea: a fair coin has no memory. Greed makes us see patterns.
- Twist: a girl tallies his coin on a wall for a week. It does have a "memory": it is weighted. Patient counting catches what quick guessing cannot.

**8.4 The Country in a Drop**
Through the new lens, a drop of pond water is a crowded country. A girl imagines herself among creatures who are sure their drop is all there is.
- Idea: scale. Being sure is not the same as being right.
- Twist: she comes back and asks if our world is somebody's drop. Then she pours the water back into the pond rather than on the floor.

**8.5 The Push That Pushed Back**
A girl is stranded on a frozen pond too slippery to walk on. No one can reach her.
- Idea: every push is answered by an equal push back. You cannot shove another without being shoved.
- Twist: she throws her heavy bag one way and slides the other way to the bank. The bully who shoved her onto the ice had rolled backwards too.

**8.6 The Address of Everything**
In a new city every house is known by two numbers counted from the mayor's house. The boy at (3, −2) is told the "minus" side is the bad side.
- Idea: a position is measured from a chosen starting point. Plus and minus are labels of the counter, not of the person.
- Twist: the children ask for the starting point to be moved to the well. Every sign changes, and the distance between friends stays exactly the same.

**8.7 The Glass of Upside-Down Water**
A showman turns a full glass upside down with only a card under it and says a spirit holds the water.
- Idea: air presses on everything. A wonder explained is still a wonder.
- Twist: a girl explains it and frees a jammed lid the same way. The showman changes his act: "It is a trick. Find it." The crowds get bigger.

**8.8 The Three Keys**
A children's club gives its kindest member everything: she writes the rules, spends the money and settles the quarrels.
- Idea: no one should hold all the keys, however good. Power needs someone else to check it.
- Twist: things go wrong with no bad intent; she simply cannot see her own mistakes. She is the one who proposes three keys in three hands.

**8.9 The Town That Wore White**
A proud town wears only white and has banned colour as impure. A glass seller's daughter hangs a prism in the square.
- Idea: white light is every colour together. What looks simple and pure may be many things joined.
- Twist: the town says the glass adds the colours. She spins a painted wheel and the colours turn back into white: proof in both directions.

**8.10 The Promise Pool**
In a fishing village, one lost boat ruins a family. The children propose that every family put in one coin a month for whoever loses a boat.
- Idea: sharing risk: a small sure cost against a rare ruin. Helping each other can be plain good sense.
- Twist: one man refuses to join and another fakes a loss. The pool survives by adding witnesses, and the boat it buys is the one that rescues the man who refused.

**8.11 The Animal With Two Hundred Names**
A girl must put the creatures of the valley in order. The bat: bird or beast? The whale: fish?
- Idea: sort by what a thing truly is, not by how it looks or what it is called.
- Twist: her system works so well that her friends try it on their club, which had sorted children by their clothes. It falls apart at once.

**8.12 The Count That Proved a Circle**
The great book says the body makes new blood from food all day. A girl counts heartbeats and measures how much each beat moves.
- Idea: a simple sum can show that a belief is impossible. Numbers outrank famous names.
- Twist: in one hour the heart moves more than a barrel. No one eats that much, so the same blood must go round and round. A band tied on her arm shows which way.

## Stage 9: revolutions (class 8; three to four pages)

**9.1 The Average Child Who Did Not Exist**
A town builds its school for the average child: average desk, average shoe, average lunch. Nothing fits anyone.
- Idea: an average can hide everyone it is made from. Look at the middle and the spread, and see each person.
- Twist: the mayor boasts that the average income doubled. A girl shows that one rich man moved in, and that the family in the middle earns what it always did.

**9.2 Nothing Is Lost**
A boy thinks a burned log is simply gone. He seals a candle in a jar on a balance, and the weight does not change.
- Idea: matter is never lost; it only goes somewhere else. So does everything we throw away.
- Twist: the dye works says the river "takes away" its waste. The children follow the colour downstream, and it ends in the owner's own well.

**9.3 The Scratch That Saved**
A healer offers a small scratch that prevents a deadly illness. Rumour says it is poison, and one father shouts loudest against it.
- Idea: a small chosen harm can prevent a great one. Decide by counting what happened, not by fear.
- Twist: the girl does not mock him. She shows him the counts, house by house. He becomes the one who convinces the next village, because he knows the fear.

**9.4 The Rules Before the Lots**
Children founding a camp on an island must write its rules before drawing lots for who will be captain, cook and cleaner.
- Idea: a rule is fair if you would accept it without knowing your place. This is the root of equal rights.
- Twist: the boy who wrote the hard rule for cleaners draws "cleaner". The camp allows one rewrite, and then makes "rules first, lots after" its permanent custom.

**9.5 The Price of Water**
A young trader finds thirsty travellers in the desert and sees a chance to charge a fortune for water.
- Idea: price and worth are different things. A deal is not fair when the other side has no choice.
- Twist: next season he is the one stranded, at their well. They charge him the ordinary price. He works out that their trust is worth more than his fortune would have been.

**9.6 The Tree That Ate Air**
A boy believes trees eat soil. He weighs a pot of soil, grows a tree in it for five years and weighs again.
- Idea: careful weighing overturns the obvious: the tree is built from air, water and light. We and the plants live on each other's breath.
- Twist: the soil has hardly changed. A candle in a sealed jar goes out; with a green plant beside it, it burns on.

**9.7 The Three Hills**
A survey team must find the height of a peak no one can reach, using only angles from three hills. A boy is tired and makes up one reading.
- Idea: the far can be known from the near, if every small measure is honest. A small lie grows.
- Twist: on the finished map, his error puts a village in the middle of a river. He owns up, and the corrected sums show the peak is the highest ever measured.

**9.8 The Engine and the Weaver**
A steam loom arrives. The weavers who will lose their work want to smash it. The engine builder's daughter and a weaver's son are friends.
- Idea: a change has winners and losers, and fairness means counting both. Neither "smash it" nor "bad luck for you" is an answer.
- Twist: the loom cannot make patterns. The weavers' knowledge, punched into cards, teaches it. The skill moves instead of dying, and the story admits what is still lost.

**9.9 One Way to Break a Number**
In a town of locksmiths, every lock has a number and opens only with its prime keys. A thief boasts of a second set of keys for lock 1001.
- Idea: every number breaks into primes in exactly one way. Integrity means being the same from every side.
- Twist: his "different" keys are 7, 11 and 13 with new labels. Since no second set can exist, the town uses the idea to make marks that cannot be forged.

**9.10 Only When It Moves**
A bookbinder's apprentice, self-taught, holds a magnet near a coil of wire. Nothing. He gives up and pulls it away, and the needle jumps.
- Idea: look for what changes. Stillness produces nothing here; movement does.
- Twist: the learned gentlemen will not listen to an apprentice. When the lighthouse lamp fails, his hand-cranked spark is what signals the ships.

**9.11 The Empty Street**
The town's first photograph shows the market street completely empty at noon. It is used as proof that a shoeshine boy was not where he says.
- Idea: evidence has a frame and a method. Ask what it could not record.
- Twist: the plate needed so long that everyone moving was wiped away. The one figure it kept is the boy, standing still at his box, exactly where he said.

**9.12 The Lord's Foot**
In one town cloth is measured by the lord's foot. The old lord dies, his son has small feet, and every length of cloth shrinks.
- Idea: a fair measure must belong to no one and be the same for all. Equality starts with a shared ruler.
- Twist: each town offers its own lord's foot instead. The children propose a length taken from something every town shares, and all the towns can at last trade.

## Stage 10: empires and freedom (class 9; four pages)

**10.1 The Empty Chair**
A new teacher seats her class by a pattern only she knows, and leaves one chair empty. "Someone is missing. I can tell you about her."
- Idea: a good theory dares to predict what has not been found, and leaves an honest gap instead of forcing a fit.
- Twist: the new student arrives and matches the predictions, except one. The teacher corrects her chart, not the girl.

**10.2 The Well and the Blamed Woman**
Sickness spreads in a town. People blame a woman who lives alone, because her house is untouched.
- Idea: mark every case on a map and look for the cause. Never blame a person without evidence.
- Twist: the marks cluster round one well. Her house was spared because she boils her water for tea: the very habit that made her seem strange.

**10.3 The White Calf**
Two brown cows have a white calf. The village says the seller cheated. A boy digs through three generations of the herd's records.
- Idea: a trait can hide for a generation and return, in a ratio you can count. We carry gifts we cannot see.
- Twist: he predicts about one white calf in four from such pairs. Three seasons prove him right and the seller's name is cleared.

**10.4 The Well Tax**
A new rule makes a town pay for water from its own wells. The children see three choices: obey, fight, or draw water openly and accept the punishment.
- Idea: open, peaceful refusal makes an unjust rule visible and leaves it no good move. The means shape the end.
- Twist: so many are arrested that the jail holds half the town. Then the jailer's own daughter walks to the well with her pot.

**10.5 The Thick-Beaked Chick**
On a small island one chick has a beak thicker than the rest and is always last at the soft seeds. Then comes a drought.
- Idea: variety plus time shapes living things, with no plan. Difference is a store of answers.
- Twist: only hard seeds are left and the thick beaks live. Then wet years return and the thin beaks do better. Nothing is "best" for ever.

**10.6 The Voice in the Wax**
A girl records her grandmother's song on a wax cylinder. By chance it also holds a landlord's promise, which he now denies.
- Idea: what is said stays said. Speak as if your words were kept.
- Twist: the same cylinder holds an unkind remark of her own. She can cut it or play all of it. She plays all of it.

**10.7 The Run on the Bank**
A rumour says the town bank is failing. Everyone rushes to take their money out, which is the one thing that can make it fail.
- Idea: some beliefs make themselves true. Trust is a shared thing that panic destroys.
- Twist: a girl walks to the front of the line and deposits her savings, and the baker announces he still takes the bank's notes. The line melts. The rumour began as her own joke, misheard.

**10.8 The Umpire Nobody Wanted**
The lane's cricket team throws out the umpire and the rules "for freedom". Within a week there is no game left, only arguments.
- Idea: rules make the game. Freedom needs limits that everyone has agreed to, and a judge who gains nothing.
- Twist: they bring the rules back with a new one: everyone takes a turn as umpire. And for sharing the one good bat, they find "you cut, I choose" again.

**10.9 The Spoiled Dish**
A careless assistant leaves a dish open and mould ruins the experiment. She could quietly throw it away.
- Idea: notice the thing that does not fit instead of hiding it. An honest report of failure is where discovery starts.
- Twist: she confesses, expecting to be sent away. Around the mould, nothing harmful has grown. The confession is the discovery.

**10.10 The Road With No Gaps**
In the Kingdom of Fractions every house has a number, and between any two houses there is always another. The people are sure nobody is left out.
- Idea: infinity inside a line, and numbers that are not fractions. Our list of "everyone" is never complete.
- Twist: a traveller with no house number arrives from between the houses. A count shows that the unnumbered outnumber the citizens.

**10.11 The Wave Nobody Owned**
A young woman finds a way to send a signal through walls. A businessman offers a fortune if she keeps it secret and his alone.
- Idea: knowledge is not used up by sharing. Weigh honestly what is gained and lost by giving it away.
- Twist: she publishes. A stranger abroad improves her design, and that improved set carries the call that saves her brother's ship.

**10.12 The Half That Was Not Asked**
A village votes on where to put its one new pump. Only the men vote, and they put it by the road.
- Idea: a decision that leaves out the people it affects is not only unfair, it is badly informed. One person, one vote.
- Twist: a girl counts the steps the water carriers now walk each day. Everyone votes again, the pump moves, and the men find they gain as well.

## Stage 11: our own time (class 10; four to five pages)

**11.1 Two Friends in Two Rooms**
Two friends are accused of copying in an exam and questioned in separate rooms. Each is told, "Blame the other and you go free."
- Idea: what is best for each alone can be worst for both. Trust and a known good name change the game.
- Twist: both are innocent and both simply tell the truth. Their accounts match in every detail: truth is the only story two people can tell without practising.

**11.2 The Loan That Grew at Night**
A boy takes a phone loan on an app: "only ₹99 a day". His sister sits down with a pencil.
- Idea: compound growth, and the real cost hidden in small print. Contentment is also arithmetic.
- Twist: her sum uncovers a fee no one was told about. Her complaint wins refunds for the whole colony, and she puts the same compounding to work in a savings account.

**11.3 The Picture That Went Round the World**
A frightening photo reaches every phone in the neighbourhood by evening. Shops shut and a crowd gathers.
- Idea: check the source before you pass anything on. A forward is your own statement.
- Twist: a girl traces it: an old picture from another country. Her correction travels far slower than the lie, so she rewrites it as a joke people want to share.

**11.4 The Machine That Knew Only Yes and No**
A girl builds a machine from switches that can add, sort and choose. Her school lets it pick the scholarship list from past years' data.
- Idea: everything a computer does is built from yes and no. It repeats what it is fed, so the responsibility stays with people.
- Twist: it picks the same kind of student as always, with perfect accuracy. She does not repair the machine. She changes the question.

**11.5 The Last Fish in the Lake**
A hundred families fish one lake. The catch falls every year, and everyone blames a different thief.
- Idea: live on the yearly growth, not on the stock. We owe a duty to those who come after us.
- Twist: the children's public ledger shows there is no thief: each family took "just one more". The limit they agree is the grown-up form of the old field and its goats.

**11.6 The Class Constitution**
A class writes its own constitution. The first thing the majority wants to vote on is banning one unpopular boy from the playground.
- Idea: some rights cannot be voted away. That is why there are rules about changing the rules.
- Twist: on the next question, the boy who proposed the ban finds himself alone against the rest, and reaches for the very right he tried to remove.

**11.7 Whose Mango Tree**
Two farmers both claim a seedling with the sweetest fruit in the district. Each has witnesses ready to swear.
- Idea: every cell carries a written record of its parents. All living things are kin.
- Twist: the test shows the tree is a cross between one orchard's tree and the other's, made by bees. Neither man lied, and they share it.

**11.8 The Group of Ten**
Ten women with nothing to pledge take a small loan together, each answerable for the others. Then one falls ill and cannot pay.
- Idea: trust can stand in for property. Sometimes rule and compassion must both be kept.
- Twist: they neither excuse her nor drop her. The nine lend to her inside the group. The bank officer who doubted them asks to copy their ledger.

**11.9 The View From Above**
Two villages have fought for years over a river that keeps shifting. A student gets ten years of satellite pictures of it.
- Idea: stepping back shows the whole. Shared, open evidence ends quarrels that argument cannot.
- Twist: from above, both villages' fields are drying for one and the same reason, far upstream. Neither was the enemy.

**11.10 The Cheapest Shirt**
A boy is proud of a bargain shirt. For a school project he follows it back through six countries to a girl of his own age who stitched it.
- Idea: every purchase is the end of a long chain of people. Some questions have no clean answer.
- Twist: refusing to buy would cost her the job. He writes to the company with what he found. She writes back. The ending is partly good, and honest about the rest.

**11.11 What the Village Counted**
A village measures its progress by one number on a board in the square: income. It rises every year. A student doing a survey counts other things too: who can read, who is healthy, who has clean water.
- Idea: what you choose to measure decides what you become. Wealth is a means, not the goal.
- Twist: income has doubled, yet the sick still travel forty kilometres and girls still leave school early. The village adds three numbers to the board, and its decisions change.

**11.12 The Lock Anyone Can Close**
A girl sells pickles online to buyers she will never meet. Her uncle asks how a stranger's payment can be safe when the whole world can see the wire.
- Idea: a lock that anyone can snap shut but only one key can open. Some sums are easy one way and nearly impossible backwards. Trust without meeting.
- Twist: a caller says, "Tell me the code so that I can send you money." She reasons that receiving never needs her key, and warns the whole lane.

## Stage 12: class 11 (five to six pages)

**12.1 The Inn With No Last Room**
An inn on an endless road has endless rooms, and every one is taken. A tired traveller knocks at midnight.
- Idea: infinity does not behave like a big number. Matching one to one, as with pebbles and goats long ago, is how we compare what cannot be counted.
- Twist: the innkeeper's daughter asks every guest to move one room along, and room 1 is free. Then a bus with endless passengers pulls in, and she still finds a way.

**12.2 The Fastest Slide**
A class is asked what shape of slide takes a ball from the roof to the far gate in the least time. Everyone is sure: a straight line.
- Idea: the shortest path is not always the fastest. Finding the best among endless choices, by trying and then by reasoning.
- Twist: they build and time shape after shape, and a dipping curve is quickest. Stranger still, on that curve a ball started half-way down arrives at the same moment as one started at the top.

**12.3 How Fast Is Now?**
A cyclist fined for speeding argues: "At any single instant I cover no distance in no time. Nothing divided by nothing. Prove my speed."
- Idea: speed at an instant is what the averages close in on as the time shrinks. The truth can live in the approach.
- Twist: a student judge takes his own trip record and halves the interval again and again in front of him. The numbers settle on one value, and he pays.

**12.4 The Test That Said Yes**
A test that is right 99 times in 100 says a boy has a rare illness. His family is sure the worst has come.
- Idea: when a thing is rare, most alarms are false. Ask how common it was to begin with. Do not condemn on one sign.
- Twist: his sister does the sum: of every hundred people the test flags, only a few are ill. A second test clears him. She then uses the same sum to defend a classmate flagged by a "cheating detector".

**12.5 The Engine That Asked for Nothing**
An inventor shows a wheel that turns for ever with no fuel and invites the town to invest its savings.
- Idea: energy is never made from nothing, and some of it always spreads out as waste. If it sounds free, it is not.
- Twist: a girl says she need not find the trick to know there is one. Then she finds it anyway: a wound spring behind the panel.

**12.6 The Marble That Used Both Doors**
In a lab, the smallest things, sent one at a time towards two slits, build a pattern as if each went through both. When the students watch which slit, the pattern disappears.
- Idea: at the smallest scale the world ignores common sense. Evidence outranks intuition, and "nobody can picture it yet" is an honest answer.
- Twist: the sums that nobody can picture predict the result to ten decimal places. The students leave less certain and far more curious.

**12.7 Whose Clock Is Right?**
Two perfect clocks are set together. One stays on the ground; the other flies round the Earth. They come back disagreeing.
- Idea: time is something measured, and it depends on motion. Two honest clocks can differ.
- Twist: the proof is already in her pocket. The map on her phone would drift by kilometres a day if the satellites' clocks were not corrected for exactly this.

**12.8 The First Domino**
A visitor "proves" that everyone in the class shares one birthday: true for one person, and if true for any group, then true for a group one bigger.
- Idea: proving endlessly many cases with two steps, and why both steps must hold. Check the first link hardest.
- Twist: every later step is sound. The proof breaks only in going from one person to two, the step nobody thought to look at.

**12.9 The Number That Was Not on the Line**
A riddle about the area of a field can be solved only by passing through the square root of a minus number. The class calls it cheating.
- Idea: an "impossible" idea that gives right answers deserves a place. Sometimes the answer is in a direction you were not looking.
- Twist: a girl shows it as a quarter turn: turn twice and you face backwards, which is minus one. It was never on the number line; it was beside it, like the finger that entered the flat world.

**12.10 Three Friends, No Favourite**
Three friends vote on where to go. Most prefer the hills to the sea, most prefer the sea to the city, and most prefer the city to the hills.
- Idea: a group of sensible people can hold a circular choice. No voting method is perfect, so fairness also needs agreed rules and good will.
- Twist: whoever sets the order of voting decides the winner. The friend who discovers this is the one holding the list, and she tells the others.

**12.11 One Letter in Three Billion**
A boy learns that his family carries "bad blood": one changed letter in the body's long code, which can cause illness.
- Idea: a trait is good or bad only in a setting. Nobody is to blame for what they inherit.
- Twist: the same single letter protected his ancestors from a deadly fever. They lived because of it, and so he exists.

**12.12 The Question With No Answer Key**
The last question of the last exam: "Is it ever right to break a promise?" Two friends write opposite answers.
- Idea: when two duties pull apart, reason it through: least harm, and would I want this as a rule for everyone? Judgment over rule following.
- Twist: both get full marks, for the reasoning. The teacher admits she has no answer key, and the learner is invited to write the next story.
