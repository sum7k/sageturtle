# Jeevani (Lives) tradeoffs (choices Claude made, 2026-11-28)

- 12 people in each of 12 groups (144). sumeet moved from 11 to 12 everywhere on 2026-11-28; the list was 121 before.
- There are only 11 Gyan subjects, so the twelfth group is my suggestion: Courage, lives that met a hard wall (illness, injury, poverty, unfair rules). sumeet may rename or replace it.
- About half India, half world (74 and 70). Women are 51 of 144; history records fewer women, so I did not force half.
- Mostly people who have died, so the story is complete. 8 are living: athletes, climbers, an astronaut and a dancer.
- Left out: founders of religions, living politicians, and rulers or leaders whose memory divides people today (for example Akbar, Shivaji, Nehru, Periyar, M.F. Husain, Henry Ford, Muhammad Yunus). sumeet can add any back.
- Kept Gandhi, Ambedkar, Ashoka, Vivekananda and Tyagaraja as widely respected.
- Savitribai and Jyotirao Phule share one life; the Wright brothers share one.
- No comparison (the spirit in CLAUDE.md): the list is in order of time inside a group, not rank. Notes avoid "first", "best" and "greatest" where the fact can be told without them. The lives will not say "be like this person".

## Writing the lives (2026-11-28)

- One file per group in `docs/lives/` (for example `01-body.md`), 12 lives each, 300 to 400 words.
- Written for the ear: short sentences, plain words, few dates and numbers.
- No "be like this person" and no closing moral to the learner. A life ends on what the person did or valued.
- Medals and records are told as plain facts, briefly. The weight is on practice, setbacks and the people who helped.
- Hard parts of a life (Partition, slavery, illness, death) are told gently and briefly, not hidden.
- Only well-documented facts. Stories that are often told but doubtful are left out (for example Dhyan Chand and Hitler). Facts are from Claude's own knowledge; a source-by-source web check is in the backlog.
- Each life ends with a "Links" line naming the Gyan subjects it touches.
